AMY Electric's website is in strong shape: 306 pages, working contact forms, and healthy search markup throughout. The main gap is off the website entirely, which is why the one action this week happens on Google, not on the site. This report covers that action, six small fixes, and where to focus content next.

Where the site stands

The website itself is ready to rank. Every page was checked directly: titles, descriptions (the short summaries Google can show under each result), canonical addresses (the single official address of each page that tells Google which copy counts), phone number, call buttons, and the structured data (machine-readable labels that help Google understand each page) all validated, with zero broken markup across 1,360 checked blocks.

What is missing lives outside the website. There is no recorded backlink or review-platform footprint under the business's control beyond its own site, so Google leans entirely on on-page signals. Claiming the Google Business Profile and collecting reviews is the highest-leverage move available.

Claim the Google Business Profile for AMY Electric and get three customer reviews this week.

For a local electrician, the map results at the top of Google are driven by the Business Profile more than by the website. Reviews there compound: each one makes the listing more visible and more trusted.

Small fixes, in order

Match opening hours everywhere easy

The site says Mon to Fri 7 to 5 and Sat 8 to 2. Google compares this against the Business Profile and directories, and mismatches erode trust.

Fix: when setting up the profile and each directory listing below, enter those exact hours every time.

List the business in three directories easy

No directory listings were found for the business. Directories are simple listings (name, address, phone, website) that confirm the business is real.

Fix: create free listings on Yelp, Angi, and the Better Business Bureau using this exact text everywhere: AMY Electric, 20628 Londelius St, Winnetka, CA 91306, (818) 302-5614, https://amyelectric.com.

Test the site on a phone easy

Most customers will arrive on mobile. The site has a gold call bar pinned to the bottom of phone screens and tap-to-call links such as tel:18183025614.

Fix: open the homepage on your phone, tap the call bar, tap Call (818) 302-5614 in the hero section, and submit the quote form with a test message. If all three work, mobile lead capture is confirmed.

Post on the Business Profile weekly easy

Profiles with recent photos and posts get more calls. The site gallery already holds 30 finished-project photos ready to reuse.

Fix: every week, post one job photo with one sentence (what was done, which neighborhood) and the phone number. Ten minutes, same habit as the review requests.

Recheck the license link yearly easy

Pages link to the state license lookup for C-10 #981578, which builds trust. Government sites change addresses over time.

Fix: once a year, click the CSLB license link in any blog author bio and confirm it still opens the license lookup page.

Watch search coverage monthly needs a free account

Google Search Console (free) reports pages Google cannot read and the searches that show the site. Nothing here needs daily attention.

Fix: verify the site at search.google.com/search-console, submit the sitemap file (the master list of pages, at https://amyelectric.com/sitemap.xml), then glance at the Coverage tab once a month for new errors.

Where to focus first

Electric-vehicle charging is the site's strongest topic: installation guides, cost breakdowns, and rebate explainers already exist and carry detailed answers. Build outward from that strength with pages that show real work rather than explain concepts.

Filmed install walkthroughs high intent

Homeowners comparing installers watch short videos of real jobs. Competitors embed technician videos with names and reviews; this site has photos but no video.

Make: three 60 to 90 second phone clips (a panel upgrade, an EV charger install, a troubleshooting diagnosis), posted to YouTube and embedded on the matching service pages.

Rebate and cost refresh posts research intent

Cost and rebate questions drive the most practical searches, and utility programs change terms. Dated pricing is the fastest way to lose trust.

Make: one short post per quarter confirming current LADWP rebate terms and installed cost ranges, linked from the existing EV charging guides.

City-named project posts local intent

Searchers include city names when hiring. The site already has a gallery page per city with real photos.

Make: one post per month featuring a finished job (city, work performed, one photo, customer quote with permission), linked from that city's gallery page.
