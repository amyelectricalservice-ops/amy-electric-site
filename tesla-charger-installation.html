FAQ

Tesla Charger FAQ

Do I need a permit for a Tesla charger in Los Angeles? ▾
Yes — we handle all permitting and inspections as part of every installation. We manage the entire process: permit applications with LADBS (Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety), plan review submissions, coordination with LADWP for service upgrades, scheduling inspections, and obtaining the final Certificate of Completion. The permit process typically takes 3-10 business days, depending on the scope of work. Permitting ensures code compliance and protects your insurance.
Can I install a Tesla charger myself? ▾
DIY EV charger installation is risky and often violates local code. Improper wiring, wrong breaker sizing, or missing GFCI protection can create fire hazards. Professional installation ensures safety, code compliance, and warranty validity for both the charger and your home insurance.
Do I need a panel upgrade to install a Tesla Wall Connector? ▾
Not always. We perform a free load calculation to determine if your existing panel can support a new circuit. Many homes can add a single EV charger or appliance without a panel upgrade if they have 200A service and available breaker slots. Older homes with 100A service typically need an upgrade to 200A. Factors include your current electrical load, the new circuit requirements, and local code requirements. We inspect at no cost and let you know before providing a final quote.
How long does a Tesla charger installation take? ▾
Most residential Level 2 installations take 2-4 hours for a straightforward install with existing 240V service nearby. If a new circuit needs to be run from the panel, add 1-2 hours. If a panel upgrade is needed, plan for a full day. Permitting adds 5-10 business days to the overall timeline. We schedule the installation once permits are approved and typically complete it in one visit.
What's the difference between a Tesla Wall Connector and a NEMA 14-50? ▾
The Tesla Wall Connector is a hardwired unit that charges up to 44 miles per hour (60A circuit). A NEMA 14-50 outlet supports up to 30 miles per hour (50A circuit) using the Mobile Connector that comes with your Tesla. The Wall Connector is faster and more convenient for daily use; the NEMA 14-50 is portable and works with any EV.
