FAQ

Tesla Charger FAQ

Do I need a permit for a Tesla charger in Los Angeles? ▾
Yes — all Tesla Wall Connector installations in Los Angeles require an electrical permit through the local building department, and AMY Electric handles the entire permitting process for every Tesla charger we install. The permit typically costs $75 to $200 and covers the plan review, rough-in inspection of the wiring and conduit, and final inspection of the completed installation. Permitting ensures your Tesla charger installation meets all current California Electrical Code requirements, including proper 60-amp circuit sizing with 6 AWG or 4 AWG wire depending on run length, GFCI protection requirements (the Wall Connector has integrated GFCI, but code requirements vary by jurisdiction), correct conduit sizing and fill, and load calculations verifying your panel can handle the additional 48-amp continuous draw. An unpermitted Tesla charger can cause problems when selling your home, as it is considered an unpermitted electrical modification that must be disclosed to potential buyers.
Can I install a Tesla charger myself? ▾
While the Tesla Wall Connector installation manual provides detailed instructions, installing a Tesla charger yourself carries significant electrical and safety risks. The installation requires a dedicated 60-amp circuit breaker (which can be hardwired at up to 48 amps continuous draw), correctly sized wiring (6 AWG for shorter runs or 4 AWG for longer runs to manage voltage drop), conduit of the correct diameter and type for the installation environment, proper torque specifications for all terminal connections (the Tesla Wall Connector requires specific torque values for the line and ground terminals), and configuration of the charger's DIP switches or software settings for the correct circuit breaker rating. Mistakes can result in overheating connections, nuisance tripping, reduced charging speed, or electrical fire. Additionally, permitting and inspection are required, and an inspector will check the installation's code compliance. Given the risks and complexity, professional installation by an EVITP-certified electrician is strongly recommended.
Do I need a panel upgrade to install a Tesla Wall Connector? ▾
Whether you need a panel upgrade depends on your home's existing electrical service capacity and your current electrical loads. The Tesla Wall Connector on a 60-amp circuit draws a continuous 48 amps (11.5 kW), which is a significant load that many older Los Angeles homes with 100A service may not be able to accommodate in addition to their existing loads. We perform a load calculation per NEC Article 220 as part of every Tesla charger estimate, adding up your home's existing lighting, appliance, and HVAC loads plus the new 48-amp EV charger load. If the total exceeds 80% of your main breaker rating (80 amps for a 100A service), you will need a panel upgrade to 200A before installing the Tesla charger. Many homes with 100A service that have gas appliances and no electric HVAC can accommodate a Tesla charger without a panel upgrade. We provide this analysis during your free on-site evaluation so you know exactly what is needed.
How long does a Tesla charger installation take? ▾
A straightforward Tesla Wall Connector installation where the charger is located in a garage within 25 to 50 feet of the electrical panel with accessible attic or crawlspace above typically takes 2 to 4 hours. If you also need a panel upgrade to accommodate the Tesla charger, the total project extends to 1 to 2 days — one day for the panel upgrade and a second day for the Tesla charger installation and testing. Installations requiring longer wiring runs, conduit through finished walls, or trenching for outdoor installations add 1 to 3 hours. After the physical installation, we configure the charger through the Tesla app, register the device with Tesla for warranty purposes, test the charging functionality, and demonstrate how to use scheduled charging and power-sharing features.
What's the difference between a Tesla Wall Connector and a NEMA 14-50? ▾
The Tesla Wall Connector is a hardwired, permanent charging station that provides the fastest possible home charging for Tesla vehicles — up to 44 miles of range per hour at 48 amps on a 60-amp circuit. It includes smart features like scheduled charging, power sharing between multiple Wall Connectors, load management, and Wi-Fi connectivity for firmware updates. The Wall Connector also supports Tesla's Universal connector (the newer J3400/NACS standard) and comes with a 4-year warranty. A NEMA 14-50 outlet is a standard 240-volt, 50-amp RV-style receptacle that requires the Tesla Mobile Connector (the portable charging cable that comes with the car) with a 14-50 adapter. It delivers up to 30 miles of range per hour — about 30% slower than the Wall Connector. The NEMA 14-50 option is less expensive (no charger purchase needed if you already have the Mobile Connector) and provides flexibility to use the same outlet for other equipment, but lacks the smart features and weather resistance of the Wall Connector.
