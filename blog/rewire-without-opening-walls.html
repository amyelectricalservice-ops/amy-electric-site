Can You Rewire a House
Without Opening the Walls?

By Amy, Licensed C-10 Electrician · 15+ years serving Los Angeles

Many homeowners delay rewiring because they fear a demolition-level mess. The good news: modern techniques let us replace unsafe wiring with surprisingly little wall damage.

Explainer

Can You Rewire a House Without Opening the Walls?

Often, yes - modern electricians can replace wiring with minimal wall damage using fishing, surface raceways, and access points. Full "no-cut" rewires are rare, but low-impact methods can eliminate most drywall work.

🔨

Cable Fishing

New NM cable is pulled through wall cavities from the attic or basement using fish tape, reusing existing routes where possible.

🕗

Surface Raceways

Low-profile conduit runs along baseboards or crown molding - ideal for finished rooms where fishing is impossible.

📎

Strategic Access

A few small openings at outlets and the attic are often enough; we patch and paint to match.

⚠

When Walls Must Open

Plaster walls, fire-blocking, or completely inaccessible runs may require limited opening. We map this before starting.

Feasibility

When Fishing Works — and When It Doesn't

Whether a low-impact rewire is possible is determined by the construction of the house, not by the electrician's skill. These are the four factors that decide it:

Wall construction

Drywall over wood studs is the easy case — fish tape travels well, and a few small openings at outlets and switches are enough. Plaster over wood lath is harder: the lath is fragile, the cavities are shallower, and fishing can crack the finish. Plaster over metal lath or wire mesh is worse still, and sometimes cannot be fished at all.

Attic and crawlspace access

Fishing depends on a path. A house with a full attic and a vented crawlspace can be rewired with almost no wall openings. A house on a slab with a finished attic has no vertical path, and the wire has to be fished horizontally through walls or run in surface raceways.

Fire blocking

Horizontal fire blocks between studs stop fish tape cold. They are required by code in most LA construction from the 1950s on, and they are the reason a "no-cut" rewire rarely survives contact with the actual house. Removing a small section of fire block through an outlet opening is normal and is patched afterward.

Insulation and obstructions

Dense-pack cellulose or spray foam fills cavities and grabs cable. Knob-and-tube that was never removed sits in the way and has to be abandoned in place. Chimneys, plumbing stacks, and HVAC ducts all take up cavity space the wire needs.

A walkthrough with a borescope before quoting tells us which of these we are dealing with. The honest answer is sometimes "this one needs limited openings" — and that is still far less invasive than a traditional rewire.

Low-impact is not cheaper — fishing takes longer than open-wall work. You are paying for the labor that keeps your walls intact. Surface raceway is cheaper but visible, and it is usually a compromise for one or two circuits rather than a whole house.

What the Inspector Checks

How a Low-Impact Rewire Passes Inspection

The method does not change the code requirement. The inspector still checks everything a traditional rewire is checked for:

  • Wire gauge and type appropriate to each circuit's load
  • Proper support and staple spacing inside cavities
  • Box fill and box type (old-work boxes where fishing requires them)
  • AFCI and GFCI protection per location
  • Grounding and bonding, including the electrode system
  • Protection of cable where it passes through framing (bushings, nail plates)

The one thing that changes is the inspection access: the inspector may ask to see specific openings or the attic path, and a borescope photo log of the routing is worth keeping. We document the route as we go so the inspection is a formality rather than a negotiation.

Questions

No-Wall Rewiring FAQ

Is a completely no-cut rewire possible? ▾
In some open-framed homes, yes. In most LA houses we make a handful of small, patched openings - far less than a traditional rewire.
Does surface conduit look bad? ▾
Modern low-profile raceways are paintable and unobtrusive, often installed along trim where they're barely noticeable.
Will fishing damage my plaster walls? ▾
Plaster is more fragile than drywall and sometimes requires small access points. We assess this during the walkthrough.
How long does a low-impact rewire take? ▾
Typically 3-7 working days for an average home, depending on accessibility and circuit count.
Is it code-compliant to rewire without opening walls? ▾
Yes, when new cable is fished through concealed spaces per NEC and passes inspection. The method doesn't change the code requirement for safe, protected wiring.
How much more does a low-impact rewire cost than a traditional one? ▾
Roughly 10 to 20 percent more labor, because fishing and minor openings take longer than open-wall work. The trade-off is that you avoid the cost and disruption of full drywall replacement and repainting, which on a plaster house can equal the electrical savings. Get both scopes quoted before deciding.
Can you rewire just one room without opening the walls? ▾
Often yes, especially if that room has attic or crawlspace access above or below it. A single-room rewire is a common scope for a kitchen remodel or a home-office upgrade, and it is a good way to test the low-impact method before committing to the whole house.
