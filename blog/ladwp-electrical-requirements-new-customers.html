LADWP Electrical Requirements
for New Customers

By Amy, California C-10 Contractor #981578

Opening a new LADWP account, buying an older Los Angeles home, or planning an electrical upgrade can involve both the utility and the local building department. This guide explains the handoffs so you can plan without relying on outdated fee amounts or assumptions.

Utility Rules and Building Codes Are Different

LADWP supplies electricity, manages utility-side service equipment, and publishes service standards. The Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety (LADBS), or another local authority, reviews construction permits and inspections. A project can need both conversations. A utility account by itself does not authorize a homeowner to alter a meter, service conductor, panel, or seal.

Rules, forms, fees, equipment lists, and appointment processes change. Use official LADWP resources and LADBS resources for current requirements, and ask a licensed electrician to interpret how they apply to your address and scope.

New account checklist

Before You Schedule Electrical Work

  1. Confirm the address and meter. Make sure the account is associated with the correct service address and identify whether the meter is present, energized, damaged, or inaccessible. Never remove a utility seal.
  2. Find the main disconnect and panel rating. Photograph the label from a safe position. Note the service size, panel brand, spare spaces, and any visible corrosion or heat damage without opening the cover.
  3. List planned loads. Include EV charging, heat pumps, air conditioning, induction cooking, solar, batteries, spas, welders, and outbuildings. A load calculation is more useful than guessing from the panel’s number.
  4. Check the jurisdiction. Los Angeles addresses generally involve LADBS, while neighboring cities may have their own department. Ask which authority issues the permit and inspection.
  5. Choose a licensed contractor. A C-10 contractor can evaluate capacity, prepare a scope, coordinate permits, and identify when LADWP must review utility-side changes.
Common projects

When LADWP or LADBS May Be Involved

Service changes and panel upgrades

A panel replacement may be straightforward, or it may require a service upgrade, new meter-main arrangement, larger conductors, a new service mast, or relocation. The electrician should verify existing equipment and submit the right information before work begins. See our panel upgrade guide for capacity questions.

New circuits and rewiring

Adding circuits, correcting old wiring, or rewiring an older home generally calls for permits and inspection. The work must be accessible for inspection and then completed safely. Avoid covering junctions or closing walls before the authority approves the rough work.

EV charger installation

A Level 2 charger normally requires a dedicated 240-volt circuit, appropriate overcurrent protection, raceway or cable, disconnecting means where required, and a load review. Some projects fit the existing service; others need a panel or service change. Our EV charger installation page covers planning, while LADWP should confirm any program-specific or utility requirements.

Solar, batteries, and generators

These systems can affect service equipment, disconnects, interconnection, and inspection sequencing. Do not energize a generator into a receptacle or panel without an approved transfer method. Confirm current utility and building-department procedures for the exact equipment.

When in doubt, pause before purchasing equipment or closing walls. A short call with the utility and the authority having jurisdiction can prevent a much more expensive correction later, particularly when service capacity or meter equipment is involved.

Coordination

How a Typical Process Works

1. Site assessment: The electrician documents the service, meter location, panel, grounding, accessible wiring, and proposed loads. Ask for photos and a written scope.

2. Design and application: The contractor selects equipment, performs calculations, and prepares permit or utility submittals. Do not rely on a verbal “should be fine” for a service change.

3. Utility review: If LADWP equipment, capacity, meter configuration, or service conductors are affected, the utility may provide requirements, a work window, or a separate inspection. The contractor should explain which party does what.

4. Construction: Work is installed according to the approved scope. Utility seals, meters, and energized equipment are handled only by authorized personnel.

5. Inspection and closeout: The authority inspects permitted construction. Correct deficiencies, obtain sign-off, and retain permits, diagrams, invoices, and equipment documentation for future owners or insurers.

Timelines depend on scope, access, jurisdiction workload, utility availability, and corrections. Avoid promising a specific date until the responsible agencies confirm it.

Avoid surprises

Questions Worth Asking Your Contractor

Who owns each step?

Ask whether the contractor, LADWP, or LADBS handles the application, disconnect, meter work, inspection, and reconnection.

What is included?

Clarify engineering, permits, trenching, drywall repair, utility coordination, equipment, testing, and final documentation.

What is not known yet?

Good estimates identify assumptions and conditions that can change after walls open or agencies review the plan.

If a new customer has flickering, an outage, or a damaged panel, use electrical repair for diagnosis rather than attempting meter or panel work. Find nearby communities on our service areas page.

Plan for handoffs

Documents to Keep With the Property

Keep the approved permit, inspection sign-off, service or panel schedule, one-line diagram when provided, equipment manuals, warranty information, and final invoice. These records help a future electrician understand what changed and help an owner answer questions from an insurer or buyer. Photograph the finished panel directory and accessible disconnects, but never photograph or publish account numbers or other private information.

For a service change, ask whether the project requires a temporary disconnect, a utility appointment, a new meter enclosure, or a change to overhead or underground conductors. Those steps can affect scheduling and may require access to a side yard, gate, alley, or interior panel. Clear the work area and confirm pets and tenants will be safe during outages.

For an EV charger, keep the charger model, circuit rating, commissioning information, and inspection record. For solar or storage, keep interconnection correspondence and operating instructions. For a generator, keep transfer-switch documentation and the safe operating procedure. These systems should never be modified by an unqualified person because incorrect changes can energize utility lines or damage equipment.

Finally, recheck official sources shortly before submitting an application. A blog can explain the process, but LADWP and LADBS control current forms, standards, fees, and appointment availability. If an agency asks for a correction, ask your contractor to explain the technical reason and the revised scope in writing.

Keep expectations realistic

Why Address-Specific Advice Matters

Two homes on the same street can have different service sizes, meter arrangements, jurisdictions, and previous permits. A new customer in a single-family home may need a simple account setup, while a remodel, ADU, multifamily property, or commercial space can involve drawings, utility review, access agreements, and multiple inspections. The safest plan starts with the actual address and equipment rather than a generic fee or timeline found online. Ask your contractor to identify assumptions and to tell you when an agency decision is still pending. Keep copies of every submission and response, and confirm appointments directly with the responsible agency. This approach does not eliminate every delay, but it makes responsibilities visible and reduces the risk of unauthorized meter work, missed inspections, or buying equipment that cannot be approved for your service.

Next step

Use Official Sources for Final Decisions

This guide is educational, not a substitute for an address-specific utility or building-department determination. Confirm the final scope before authorizing work, and ask for updated written requirements whenever the project schedule changes or equipment is substituted. Written confirmation is especially useful for service changes, EV equipment, and projects involving more than one agency or inspection. It gives everyone a shared record of the current plan and reduces avoidable confusion during scheduling.

FAQ

LADWP Requirements FAQ

What should a new customer do first? ▾
Confirm the address and meter with LADWP, identify whether service is energized, and have a licensed electrician inspect before changing equipment or loads.
Does electrical work require a permit? ▾
Many panel, service, EV charger, new-circuit, and rewiring projects require permits and inspection. Confirm with LADBS or your local authority.
Does LADWP approve a panel upgrade? ▾
Utility coordination may cover service or meter equipment while LADBS handles construction permits. Your electrician should identify both roles.
Can I add an EV charger? ▾
Yes, after a load calculation and properly designed, permitted circuit. A service or panel change may be needed.
Where are current fees listed? ▾
Check official LADWP and LADBS sources for current fees, forms, and timelines. They vary by address and project.
