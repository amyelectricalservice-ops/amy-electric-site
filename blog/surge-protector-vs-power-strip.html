Surge Protector vs Power Strip: Critical Differences

Many homeowners use the terms interchangeably — but they are NOT the same thing.

Key Differences

Key Differences at a Glance

🔌

Power Strip

Just an extension cord. NO surge protection. $10–$30. No joule rating = NOT a surge protector.

🛡️

Surge Protector (Point-of-Use)

Contains MOV components that absorb voltage spikes. Rated in joules. $20–$100.

🏠

Whole-Home Surge Protector

Installed at your panel. Protects EVERY circuit from external surges. $300–$800 installed.

⚡

UPS (Battery Backup)

Keeps devices running during brief outages. $100–$500+.

Why It Matters

Why This Matters in Los Angeles

LA experiences unique surge risks:

  • Grid fluctuations — LADWP load switching causes cumulative surges
  • PSPS events — Shutoffs and restorations create repeated power surges
  • AC cycling — Large compressors create voltage fluctuations

Look for UL 1449 rating and at least 1,000 joules for basic protection.

Frequently Asked Questions About Surge Protection

What is the difference between a surge protector and a power strip? ▾
A power strip simply provides additional outlets and acts as an extension cord with no surge protection. A surge protector contains MOV (metal oxide varistor) components that absorb voltage spikes and redirect excess energy to the ground wire. You can tell the difference by looking for a joule rating — if there is no joule rating on the device, it is not a surge protector.
Do I need a whole-home surge protector in Los Angeles? ▾
Yes. A whole-home surge protector installed at your electrical panel protects every circuit in your home, including hardwired appliances like refrigerators, HVAC systems, and washer/dryers that plug-in surge protectors cannot reach. LA experiences grid fluctuations from LADWP load switching, PSPS shutoff restorations, and AC compressor cycling that cause cumulative surge damage.
How much does whole-home surge protection cost? ▾
Whole-home surge protector installation typically costs $300 to $800 including the device, labor, and permit. This is a fraction of the cost of replacing a single damaged appliance or HVAC compressor. Point-of-use surge protectors cost $20 to $100 each and should be used in addition to a whole-home unit for layered protection.
