Surge Protection

Surge Protector vs Power Strip: Critical Differences

Many homeowners use the terms interchangeably — but they are NOT the same thing.

Key Differences

Key Differences at a Glance

🔌

Power Strip

Just an extension cord. NO surge protection. $10–$30. No joule rating = NOT a surge protector.

🛡️

Surge Protector (Point-of-Use)

Contains MOV components that absorb voltage spikes. Rated in joules. $20–$100.

🏠

Whole-Home Surge Protector

Installed at your panel. Protects EVERY circuit from external surges. $300–$800 installed.

⚡

UPS (Battery Backup)

Keeps devices running during brief outages. $100–$500+.

Why It Matters

Why This Matters in Los Angeles

LA experiences unique surge risks:

  • Grid fluctuations — LADWP load switching causes cumulative surges
  • PSPS events — Shutoffs and restorations create repeated power surges
  • AC cycling — Large compressors create voltage fluctuations

Look for UL 1449 rating and at least 1,000 joules for basic protection.

Whole-Home Protection

Whole-Home Surge Protector Installation in Los Angeles

Point-of-use surge protectors only guard what is plugged into them. A whole-home (Type 2) surge protector installed at the panel guards every circuit, including hardwired appliances and EV chargers that no power strip can protect.

What it costs

Whole-home surge protector installation typically runs $300 to $700 in Los Angeles, including the device, breaker space, and labor. Many homeowners add it during a panel upgrade or EV charger install at lower incremental cost.

When it matters most

  • Homes with a heat pump, EV charger, or induction cooktop (high-value electronics)
  • Older service panels without surge protection
  • Properties near utility substations or with frequent grid switching
  • Wildfire-adjacent areas where PSPS events and restoration surges occur
LA Scenarios

Common Surge Damage Scenarios in Los Angeles Homes

Los Angeles homes face unique surge risks beyond typical lightning strikes:

  • PSPS restoration surges. When power returns after a Public Safety Power Shutoff, voltage spikes can damage sensitive electronics.
  • Grid switching. LADWP load switching during peak demand creates transient surges.
  • AC compressor cycling. Large HVAC units generate internal surges when starting and stopping.
  • EV charger loads. High-amperage EV charging can cause voltage fluctuations on older panels.
  • Solar export. Homes with solar panels experience bidirectional power flow surges.

A whole-home surge protector is the only defense against these LA-specific risks that point-of-use units cannot cover.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About Surge Protection

What is the difference between a surge protector and a power strip? ▾
A power strip simply provides additional outlets and acts as an extension cord with no surge protection. A surge protector contains MOV (metal oxide varistor) components that absorb voltage spikes and redirect excess energy to the ground wire. You can tell the difference by looking for a joule rating — if there is no joule rating on the device, it is not a surge protector.
Do I need a whole-home surge protector in Los Angeles? ▾
Yes. A whole-home surge protector installed at your electrical panel protects every circuit in your home, including hardwired appliances like refrigerators, HVAC systems, and washer/dryers that plug-in surge protectors cannot reach. LA experiences grid fluctuations from LADWP load switching, PSPS shutoff restorations, and AC compressor cycling that cause cumulative surge damage.
How much does whole-home surge protection cost? ▾
Whole-home surge protector installation typically costs $300 to $800 including the device, labor, and permit. This is a fraction of the cost of replacing a single damaged appliance or HVAC compressor. Point-of-use surge protectors cost $20 to $100 each and should be used in addition to a whole-home unit for layered protection.
Do I still need point-of-use surge protectors if I have a whole-home unit? ▾
Yes. Whole-home units handle external surges from the grid; point-of-use units add a second layer against internal surges generated by large appliances. For home offices and entertainment centers, both is best practice.
How often should a surge protector be replaced? ▾
Point-of-use units every 3 to 5 years or after a major surge; whole-home units every 5 to 10 years or when the indicator light fails. Replacement is cheap compared to the damage a failed protector can no longer stop.
Can a surge protector protect against lightning? ▾
A whole-home surge protector reduces the risk from nearby lightning strikes, but a direct strike can overwhelm any protector. Point-of-use units add a second layer. For maximum protection, combine whole-home and point-of-use units.
Do surge protectors wear out? ▾
Yes. The MOV components degrade with each surge. Most units have an indicator light; when it fails or dims, replace the unit. AMY Electric recommends replacement every 5 to 10 years for whole-home units.
