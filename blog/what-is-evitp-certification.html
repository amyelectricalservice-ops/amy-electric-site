01 · What EVITP Means

What Is EVITP Certification?

EVITP stands for Electric Vehicle Infrastructure Training Program. It is a California-specific certification for electricians who install EV charging equipment. The program covers:

  • NEC Article 625 — the specific code section governing EV charging installations
  • EVSE load calculations — sizing circuits, breakers, and panels for charger loads
  • DC fast charging fundamentals — even though most homes get Level 2
  • Commissioning and testing — verifying the installation works safely
  • Manufacturer-specific training — Tesla Wall Connector, ChargePoint, JuiceBox, and others

The certification requires approximately 20 hours of training plus a proctored exam with a 70% passing threshold. It costs $275 and is valid for 3 years before renewal.

02 · Why It Matters

Why EVITP Certification Matters for LA Homeowners

Fewer inspection failures

EVITP-trained electricians understand LADBS inspection requirements for EV charger circuits. The inspector checks breaker sizing, conduit installation, GFCI protection, and labeling — EVITP training covers all of these.

Correct equipment selection

Not all EV chargers work in all homes. An EVITP-certified electrician evaluates your panel capacity, charger location, and daily driving habits before recommending a unit. This prevents the expensive mistake of buying a charger that your panel cannot support.

Rebate eligibility

LADWP requires a licensed C-10 contractor for the $500 rebate. EVITP certification goes further — it signals that your installer has specialized EV training, which matters for CALeVIP, SCE Charge Ready, and other utility programs that require EVITP-trained electricians.

Safety

A 240V/40-50 amp circuit in a garage or outdoor area involves significant electrical load. EVITP training emphasizes proper grounding, conductor sizing, and outdoor protection — the details that prevent fires and electrocution.

03 · EVITP vs C-10

EVITP vs. C-10 License: What Is the Difference?

These are two different credentials that serve different purposes:

CredentialWhat It CoversRequired for EV Chargers?
C-10 Electrical LicenseAll electrical work — wiring, panels, outlets, fixtures, EV chargersYes — legally required for any EV charger installation in California
EVITP CertificationSpecialized EV charging installation training — NEC Article 625, load calculations, commissioningNot legally required for private residential, but strongly recommended and required for utility-funded programs

A C-10 license without EVITP can legally install your EV charger. But an EVITP-certified electrician has proven they understand the specific requirements of EV charging — from load calculations to inspection readiness. Think of it as the difference between a general contractor and a specialist who does this work every day.

05 · When EVITP Is Required

When Is EVITP Certification Required?

Most private residential EV charger installations do not legally require EVITP — a C-10 license is sufficient. However, EVITP is required or strongly preferred in these situations:

  • CALeVIP rebates — requires EVITP-Approved contractor
  • SCE Charge Ready programs — requires EVITP-certified electrician
  • Clean Cars 4 All — requires EVITP for charger installations
  • Multi-unit dwellings — HOAs and property managers increasingly require EVITP
  • Commercial EV charging — most commercial programs require EVITP

If you plan to pursue any utility rebate beyond the basic LADWP $500, EVITP certification is not optional — it is a requirement. Even if you are not pursuing rebates now, choosing an EVITP-certified electrician future-proofs your installation.

06 · How to Verify

How to Verify an Electrician's EVITP Status

Do not take "EVITP certified" at face value. Verify it yourself in two steps:

  1. Check the contractor page: Visit evitp.org/california and look for the company name. This confirms the company is "EVITP Approved."
  2. Check the individual electrician: Search db.evitp.org by last name or certification number. This confirms the specific person installing your charger holds current certification.

EVITP certification expires after 3 years. An expired credential is as good as none — always verify current status before hiring.
