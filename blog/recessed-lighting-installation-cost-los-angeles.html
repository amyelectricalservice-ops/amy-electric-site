02 · Per-fixture breakdown

Per-Fixture Cost Breakdown

Each recessed can is priced from four components. Understanding them helps you compare quotes apples-to-apples:

  • Fixture and housing ($60–$150): IC-rated LED recessed housing with integrated trim. Wafer-thin LED panels cost less; adjustable gimbal or wet-location housings cost more.
  • Labor ($75–$150 per can): Cutting, fishing wire, mounting, and finishing. Labor dominates in LA because of high trade rates and tight attic access in older homes.
  • Trim and finish ($15–$40): Baffle, reflector, or decorative trim. Most LA homeowners choose white baffle trims for a clean look.
  • Switching and dimming ($100–$350 per location): Standard dimmer or smart dimmer, including box and wiring. One dimmer can control a whole zone of cans.

Adding a dedicated circuit for a new bank of cans runs $350–$750 and is required when the existing circuit is at capacity or when code demands it for a new load.

03 · Cost drivers

What Drives the Cost of Recessed Lighting in LA

🏠

Ceiling Type

Open attic access is fastest and cheapest. Finished ceilings with no attic access require fishing wire through walls, adding labor. Vaulted or cathedral ceilings need specialized housings.

🧱

Insulation and IC Rating

IC-rated housings are required in insulated ceilings and are standard in LA attics. Non-IC housings need clearance and are rarely used in modern installs.

⚡

Wiring Access

Older LA homes with knob-and-tube or ungrounded wiring may need circuit upgrades before new cans can be added safely.

🎛️

Switching and Dimmers

Adding a new switch location, a dimmer, or a smart control adds material and labor. Multi-zone control (kitchen + living + hallway) multiplies switching cost.

💡

Fixture Selection

Wafer LEDs are the value choice. Adjustable, wet-rated, or color-tunable housings add 20–40% to the fixture cost.

📐

Room Layout

Irregular ceilings, beams, or soffits require custom spacing and more labor per can.

04 · Permits and code

Permit and Code Considerations for Recessed Lighting in LA

Recessed lighting is not always a permit-free upgrade. The rules in Los Angeles:

  • New circuits require a permit. Adding a dedicated circuit for a new bank of cans triggers an LADBS electrical permit and a city inspection.
  • Existing-circuit swaps may not. Replacing like-for-like fixtures on an existing circuit with spare capacity sometimes does not require a permit, but the work must still meet code.
  • Title 24 applies. California's energy code requires high-efficacy (LED) fixtures and, in many cases, vacancy or dimmer controls in kitchens and bathrooms.
  • IC rating is code. Housings in insulated ceilings must be IC-rated; non-IC housings in insulated ceilings are a fire hazard and a code violation.
  • AMY Electric handles permitting. We file the LADBS permit, schedule the inspection, and attend it. Permit and inspection fees ($200–$450) are included in our all-in quotes when a permit is required.

Los Angeles uses LADBS (Department of Building and Safety) for electrical permitting, with online filing through PermitLA and ePlanLA. Never hire a contractor who suggests skipping the permit for new circuits.

07 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does recessed lighting installation cost in Los Angeles?
Recessed lighting in Los Angeles typically costs $150 to $350 per can installed, including the fixture, housing, and labor. A full room package runs $900 to $3,000 depending on fixture count, ceiling type, and switching. A standard kitchen or living room with six to eight cans lands near $1,200 to $2,400. AMY Electric provides firm written quotes after a site visit, and the LADBS permit is included when one is required.
Do I need a permit for recessed lighting in Los Angeles?
Often yes. Adding new circuits or modifying existing wiring for recessed lighting requires an LADBS electrical permit, and the finished work must pass a city inspection against the California Electrical Code. AMY Electric files the permit, schedules the inspection, and attends it. Replacing like-for-like fixtures on an existing circuit may not require a permit, but new circuits, dimmer additions, or smart controls do.
How long does recessed lighting installation take?
A typical room with six to eight cans takes four to eight hours. Larger whole-home projects run one to three days. LADBS inspection, when required, typically follows within 48 hours of completion. Accessible attic or crawlspace wiring speeds the job; finished ceilings with no attic access add time for fishing wire.
What is an IC-rated recessed light?
IC-rated means the housing is safe for direct contact with insulation. Non-IC housings require a three-inch clearance from insulation and are not allowed in insulated ceilings. In Los Angeles attics, IC-rated LED housings are the standard choice and cost roughly the same as non-IC units while meeting Title 24 and fire-safety requirements.
Can I add dimmers or smart switches to recessed lighting?
Yes. Dimmer switches add $100 to $250 per location including the switch and labor, and smart dimmers add $150 to $350. AMY Electric can pair recessed lighting with smart home electrical upgrades for app and voice control, and dedicated circuits for high-load zones.
Does recessed lighting increase home value?
Yes. Recessed lighting is one of the highest-ROI lighting upgrades for kitchens and living rooms, and it pairs well with whole-home rewiring or panel upgrades in older LA homes. A permitted installation also produces an inspection record that supports resale and insurance documentation.
