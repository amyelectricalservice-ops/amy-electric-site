01 · What a fuse box is

What a Fuse Box Is and Why LA Homes Still Have Them

A fuse box is a metal enclosure of screw-in or cartridge fuses that protects each circuit. When current exceeds what the fuse is rated for, the metal element melts and the circuit goes dead until a replacement fuse goes in. Circuit breaker panels do the same job with switches that simply reset.

Los Angeles still has thousands of homes wired this way. Our own panel replacement work identifies fuse boxes and 60A services across the city's oldest neighborhoods, including Echo Park and Highland Park homes, the same territory our panel upgrade teams cover daily. Houses built before the 1960s were wired for a fraction of today's load, and the fuse box was sized for that world, a household with no EV charger and no central air to feed.

Open the metal cabinet near the meter: round glass or cartridge fuses in rows, or a pull-out block holding cartridge fuses, means you have a fuse box. Replacement means removing the fuse box and installing a modern breaker panel; the job is a service panel replacement, not an accessory swap.

Fuse vs. Breaker

Fuses versus Breakers: What Changes

A fuse and a breaker both stop current that exceeds the circuit's safe rating. The difference is what happens next: a fuse melts and must be physically replaced, and when the household already has replacement fuses of the wrong size in a drawer, the wrong fuse ends up back in the socket, protecting nothing. A breaker is a switch: it trips, you reset it, and it protects the same circuit again.

That difference compounds on an old panel. Fuse panels age alongside their wiring, offer no room for new circuits, and provide none of the AFCI or GFCI protection that modern breaker panels install on residential circuits. Fuse boxes also predate today's load entirely: they were built when a fridge, some lights, and a radio were the whole house.

Insurance & Escrow

Fuse Panels, Insurance, and Escrow

The same insurance dynamics we document for hazardous legacy panels apply to fuse boxes: California insurers frequently decline to write new homeowner policies on houses with documented-hazard electrical equipment, and inspectors flag old fuse boxes on home inspection reports. On a sale, that flag typically turns into a buyer demand: a replacement credit, or the work completed before escrow closes.

A permitted replacement hands both sides what they need: a modern 30–42 space breaker panel and an LADBS inspection record in your files. If your fuse box homes equipment that is on the hazardous-brand list, see our Federal Pacific, Zinsco & Sylvania panel replacement page and our Sylvania Zinsco replacement guide.

EV Charger

Replacing a Fuse Box Before Installing an EV Charger

An EV charger cannot hang off a fuse panel. A Level 2 charger needs a dedicated 50A-class circuit, breaker space, and capacity the fuse box doesn't have. For EV homes the panel replacement is step one, and the charger is the step right behind it. Our published charger pricing once the panel is modern: a Tesla Wall Connector installation runs $500–$1,200, a NEMA 14-50 outlet $350–$600, and a hardwired EVSE $500–$1,200, permit included.

The efficient path is to do both as one job: when we add the charger circuit during the panel replacement, the incremental cost is typically $500 to $1,200: one permit, one utility outage, one inspection. Frequently Asked Questions About Fuse Box Replacement

How much does it cost to replace a fuse box in Los Angeles? ▾
We price fuse box replacement from the same cost table we publish for panel upgrades, because replacing a fuse panel with a modern breaker box is a service panel replacement. A 100A to 200A upgrade runs $2,500–$4,500 and covers the permit, labor, materials, and inspection. When the old equipment includes Federal Pacific- or Zinsco-class hazards, published replacement pricing with remediation is $2,800–$4,800, and a 200A-to-400A dual-service project runs $4,500–$8,000. The LADBS permit and inspection fees ($200–$450) are included in our quotes; the utility service connection, typically $300–$600, is billed separately by LADWP or SCE.
Do I need a permit to replace a fuse box in Los Angeles? ▾
Yes. A fuse box replacement is a main service panel replacement, which requires an LADBS electrical permit, and the finished work must pass a city inspection against the California Electrical Code. LADBS runs its permitting online through PermitLA and ePlanLA, and AMY Electric files the permit, schedules the inspection, and attends it for you. Never hire a contractor who suggests skipping the permit; unpermitted panel work resurfaces at the sale of the home, after insurance claims, and whenever the panel is opened again.
How long does a fuse box replacement take? ▾
The physical replacement takes four to eight hours with the power off. LADBS inspection typically follows within 48 hours of completion. If LADWP or SCE must upgrade your service connection for the higher amperage, add one to three weeks of utility-side time that runs parallel to our work. From signed estimate to passed inspection, most straightforward replacements finish within one to three weeks.
Can I install an EV charger on a home with a fuse box? ▾
Not on the fuse box itself. A Level 2 charger needs a dedicated 50A-class circuit and a service panel with spare capacity and breaker space, and a fuse panel has neither, so the panel replacement comes first. Once the modern panel is in, a Tesla Wall Connector is $500–$1,200 installed, a NEMA 14-50 outlet is $350–$600, and a hardwired EVSE is $500–$1,200. Adding the EV charger circuit during the panel job is the efficient path: when we add a charger circuit during a panel upgrade, the incremental cost is typically $500 to $1,200. See our LADWP EV charger rebate guide for up to $1,000 back.
Does an old fuse panel affect home insurance or a home sale? ▾
It can. Our published guidance on hazardous panels applies here: insurers in California frequently refuse to write new homeowner policies on houses with documented-hazard equipment, and inspectors flag old fuse boxes and legacy panels on inspection reports, with buyers commonly demanding a replacement credit or completed replacement before escrow closes. After a permitted replacement you have modern equipment and an inspection record to hand both your insurer and escrow.
Can I keep the fuse box or just swap in bigger fuses? ▾
No, and never up-rate a fuse past the circuit it protects. A fuse box cannot be converted to breakers in place; the compliant path is a permitted panel replacement. Slower-blowing or oversized fuses let wiring cook before they ever blow, and repeated blown fuses usually point to an overloaded panel rather than weak fuses. If you smell burning or see scorching at the fuse box, treat it as an emergency and call a licensed electrician immediately.
