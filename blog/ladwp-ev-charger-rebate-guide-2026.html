LADWP EV Charger Rebate Guide 2026:
What Actually Pays, and What Ended

By Amy, Licensed C-10 Electrician · AMY Electric, Los Angeles

LADWP pays up to $1,000 toward a qualifying Level 2 home charger, and up to $1,500 if you're enrolled in Lifeline or EZ-SAVE. The federal 30C credit ended June 30, 2026, so the utility rebate is now the main incentive. Most published guides still describe an approval process that LADWP does not run.

Overview

What LADWP Actually Pays

LADWP's Residential EV Charger Rebate Program covers both the charger and the labor to install it. Eligible costs explicitly include the hardware, the electrician's labor, panels, circuit breakers, meter sockets, conduit, wiring, junction boxes, disconnects, switches, fuses, and permit costs. The rebate is capped at what you actually spent, minus any money you received from third parties.

LADWP program | ComponentAmount
Charger purchase and installationUp to $1,000
Lifeline or EZ-SAVE adder (enrolled at application)+$500
Dedicated EV TOU meter (flat)+$250

One charger rebate per customer of record per residence, and one EV TOU meter rebate per residence. Funds are released first-come, first-served.

The Rule Most Guides Get Wrong

You Apply After the Charger Is Installed

A lot of published rebate advice says you need "pre-approval" before work starts and have 120 days to finish. LADWP's own FAQ says the opposite: you must first install the qualifying charger at your residence before you can apply for any of the rebates. There is no pre-approval stage and no 120-day completion window in the current program.

In place of pre-approval there is a paperwork deadline. Apply within 12 months of the charger purchase date. If you are claiming installation costs only (say, you were given a charger) or the dedicated meter only, the window is six months from the installation date.

Repayment has its own clock. The charger has to stay in service at that address for at least six months, and LADWP says it conducts site inspections to confirm.

Eligibility

Who Qualifies

  • You are the LADWP account holder named on the bill
  • You have an active LADWP electric meter on a residential rate plan
  • The charger and installation were paid for — LADWP wants proof of payment, not an open balance
  • You have not already received a charger rebate at the same residence
  • The installation was permitted by LADBS and the permit shows Finaled status
  • The work was performed by a licensed electrician — LADWP requires the installer's license number on the invoice

LADWP verifies Lifeline and EZ-SAVE enrollment at the time it reviews your application, so the $500 adder depends on where you stand on the day you submit, not the day you installed.

Equipment

What the Charger Has to Be

LADWP is specific about hardware. A charger missing any one of these is ineligible no matter how good the install is:

1
240-volt Level 2 input

Level 1 (120V) equipment does not qualify.

2
NRTL certification at time of purchase

UL, ETL, or another nationally recognized testing laboratory recognized by OSHA.

3
Network-capable over Wi-Fi or Ethernet

A dumb charger with no network connection is out.

4
SAE J1772 or SAE J3400 connector

J3400 is the newer standard; both are accepted.

5
New and unused, purchased within 12 months

Resold, refurbished, rebuilt, warranty-claim, gifted, and prize units are excluded.

LADWP confirms that the Tesla Wall Connector and Tesla Universal Wall Connector are eligible. The program also maintains a Qualifying Product List, and it is worth checking your exact model against it before you buy — the list is revised.

Application

How to Claim the Rebate

1
Check capacity before you buy anything

A Level 2 charger needs a dedicated 240V circuit. If your panel is short on capacity, that changes the budget, and the panel work is itself an eligible rebate cost when it is part of the charger install.

2
Get the permit and install

LADBS permit, licensed electrician, inspection passed. Everything has to be signed off before the rebate packet can go in.

3
Collect the paid invoices

Charger invoice and installation invoice, both itemized and marked paid. License number on the installation invoice.

4
Photograph the nameplate

Color photos of the charger's device nameplate showing serial number, model, manufacture date, and the NRTL mark. Add a photo of the meter if you installed a dedicated one.

5
Submit within the window

Online through your LADWP account, or by mail to LADWP Residential EV Charger Rebate Program, PO Box 51111, Room 1019, Los Angeles, CA 90051-0100.

Questions go to LADWP's EV program specialists at (866) 484-0433. They will confirm your rate options and whether a dedicated meter is worth it for your driving.

Metering

The Dedicated EV TOU Meter and Rate Discount

LADWP offers an EV rate discount of $0.025 per kWh on base-period charging. To get it, your charger has to be separately metered from the house meter, and the EV meter has to sit on a time-of-use rate. There is a $10 per month minimum charge on that meter plus adjustment factors, and no service charge or ESA adjustment factor on the EV rate itself.

That is what the flat $250 rebate covers — the installation of the second meter. Whether it pays off depends on how much you drive, because you are adding a monthly minimum to save a few cents per kWh. LADWP recommends evaluating the rate against the install cost before you commit.

Federal Credit

Section 30C Ended June 30, 2026

If you have read that the federal government pays 30% of your charger up to $1,000 and the credit runs through 2032, that information is out of date. The IRS is explicit: for the Section 30C alternative fuel vehicle refueling property credit, no credit is allowed for property placed in service after June 30, 2026.

For the period it was live, an individual could claim 30% of the cost of property placed in service at a main home, capped at $1,000 per item, using Form 8911. Businesses could claim 6% up to $100,000 per item, or 30% with prevailing wage and apprenticeship compliance.

There was a second catch that most 2026 articles skipped: the property also had to sit in an eligible census tract — a low-income community or a non-urban tract. A charger in a qualifying tract installed before July 1, 2026 could generate the credit. A charger installed in October 2026 cannot, regardless of tract.

If you had a charger placed in service before the cutoff and did not claim it, that is a question for a tax professional — not for your electrician.

Other Programs

What CALeVIP Does and Does Not Cover

CALeVIP is often listed as stackable with a home charger rebate. It is not. CALeVIP funds publicly available charging infrastructure, and it is open to property owners, contractors, businesses, and tribes. A charger in a single-family garage is not a publicly available charger. Homeowners should plan around the LADWP rebate on its own rather than counting on a second incentive.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About LADWP Rebates

How much is the LADWP EV charger rebate in 2026? ▾
LADWP pays up to $1,000 toward the purchase and installation of a qualifying Level 2 charger. Customers enrolled in Lifeline or EZ-SAVE at the time of application can receive an additional $500, for up to $1,500. A dedicated EV TOU meter carries a separate flat $250 rebate. The rebate cannot exceed your actual costs minus any third-party incentives.
Do I apply for the LADWP rebate before or after installation? ▾
After. LADWP requires the charger to be installed at your residence before you can apply. Apply within 12 months of the charger purchase date, or within six months of the installation date if you are claiming installation costs only or the dedicated meter only.
Is the federal 30C tax credit still available in 2026? ▾
No. The Section 30C alternative fuel vehicle refueling property credit is not allowed for property placed in service after June 30, 2026. Chargers installed after that date no longer generate a federal credit. The credit also required an eligible low-income or non-urban census tract, so not every Los Angeles address qualified even before the deadline.
Which EV chargers qualify for the LADWP rebate? ▾
LADWP lists five requirements: 240-volt Level 2 input; certification by a nationally recognized testing laboratory such as UL or ETL at the time of purchase; network capability over Wi-Fi or Ethernet; an SAE J1772 or SAE J3400 connector; and new, unused condition. The charger must also be purchased within 12 months of your application. LADWP confirms the Tesla Wall Connector and Tesla Universal Wall Connector are eligible.
What documents does LADWP require for the rebate? ▾
An itemized, paid charger invoice showing the purchase date, retailer, make and model, the charger cost itemized separately, and proof of payment. For installation, an itemized paid invoice showing your contractor's name, address, license number and phone, the LADWP service address, the work performed, a model number matching the nameplate, and proof of payment. Plus an LADBS electrical permit with Finaled status, color photos of the charger nameplate, and a W-9 if the payee is a business.
Does the charger have to stay installed after the rebate? ▾
Yes. The charger must remain in service at the address on your application for at least six months. If you remove it earlier while your LADWP account stays active, LADWP can require you to repay the rebate.
Can I stack LADWP rebates with CALeVIP? ▾
No. CALeVIP funds publicly available charging and is open to property owners, contractors, businesses and tribes, not residential home chargers. A single-family homeowner should plan around the LADWP rebate alone.
