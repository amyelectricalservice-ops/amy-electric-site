02 · Cost drivers

What Drives Commercial EV Charging Cost in LA

🔌

Port Count

More ports mean more hardware, wiring, and trenching. Volume discounts apply.

📏

Trenching & Conduit

Distance from panel to parking, asphalt cutting, and underground conduit add cost.

⚡

Panel Capacity

Existing service may need upgrading. LADWP/SCE coordination adds time and cost.

🔋

DCFC vs Level 2

DC fast chargers need utility upgrades and cooling; Level 2 is simpler and cheaper.

📊

Load Management

Smart power sharing avoids costly service upgrades on large sites.

🏗️

Site Conditions

Indoor vs outdoor, ADA compliance, and signage affect installation scope.

03 · Rebates and ROI

Rebates, ROI, and Payback for Commercial EV Charging

  • LADWP/SCE rebates. Commercial programs cover a significant share of hardware and installation costs, often up to $4,000 per port. AMY Electric helps with the application.
  • ROI. Payback depends on utilization, pricing model (free, cost-recovery, or profit), and rebates. Most Level 2 projects pay back in 3 to 7 years.
  • Revenue models. Charge per kWh, per session, or per hour. Free charging drives tenant/employee satisfaction.
  • Property value. EV charging increases property value and attracts tenants.
04 · Load management and OCPP

Load Management and OCPP for Commercial Sites

  • Load management. Smart power sharing across ports avoids costly service upgrades and maximizes existing capacity.
  • OCPP. Open Charge Point Protocol prevents vendor lock-in and enables unified management, billing, and remote monitoring.
  • Networking. Cellular or Wi-Fi connectivity required for OCPP and payment processing.
  • Scalability. Design for future expansion — add ports without new trenching.
05 · Permits and code

Permit and Code Considerations for Commercial EV Charging in LA

  • Permit required. All commercial EV charging requires an LADBS electrical permit and inspection.
  • Utility coordination. LADWP or SCE service upgrades require utility approval and may add weeks.
  • Code compliance. Work must meet California Electrical Code and accessibility requirements.
  • EVITP-certified. AMY Electric is EVITP-certified (#4051604) for commercial EV installations.

Los Angeles uses LADBS for electrical permitting. Never hire a contractor who suggests skipping the permit.

Property Management

Property Management and Tenant Considerations for Commercial EV Charging

  • Tenant demand. EV charging is increasingly expected by tenants and employees. Properties without it lose tenants to competitors.
  • Billing models. Sub-metering, flat fees, or free charging. OCPP enables per-user billing.
  • ADA compliance. Accessible charging spaces required for public and employee parking.
  • Future-proofing. Design for 2x-3x growth; add conduit for future ports during initial trenching.
  • Maintenance. Budget 2-5% of hardware cost annually for network, software, and hardware maintenance.

07 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does commercial EV charging installation cost in Los Angeles?
Commercial EV charging in Los Angeles costs $2,000 to $8,000 per Level 2 port and $30,000 to $150,000 or more for DC fast charging (DCFC). Costs depend on port count, trenching, panel capacity, and utility. AMY Electric provides firm written quotes after a site visit.
Are there rebates for commercial EV charging in LA?
Yes. LADWP and SCE offer rebates for commercial Level 2 and DCFC installations, often covering a significant share of hardware and installation costs. AMY Electric helps with the application and documentation. Incentives change, so we confirm current programs during the quote.
What is the ROI on commercial EV charging?
ROI depends on utilization, pricing model (free, cost-recovery, or profit), and rebates. Most commercial Level 2 installations pay back in 3 to 7 years through charging revenue, tenant retention, and increased property value. DCFC payback varies with utilization.
What is OCPP and do I need it?
OCPP (Open Charge Point Protocol) is an open standard that lets chargers from different manufacturers talk to a single management platform. It prevents vendor lock-in and enables load management, billing, and remote monitoring. AMY Electric recommends OCPP-compliant chargers for commercial projects.
Do I need a panel upgrade for commercial EV charging?
Often yes. Multiple Level 2 ports or a DCFC unit can exceed existing panel capacity. AMY Electric performs a load calculation and coordinates with LADWP/SCE for service upgrades when needed.
How long does commercial EV charging installation take?
A small Level 2 project (2–4 ports) takes 1 to 3 weeks from permit to completion. Larger projects with trenching, panel upgrades, or DCFC take 4 to 12 weeks. AMY Electric phases work to minimize business disruption.
Should I offer free or paid charging for tenants?
It depends on your goals. Free charging attracts tenants and employees; paid charging generates revenue. Many properties start free to build demand, then transition to cost-recovery pricing. OCPP enables flexible billing.
How do I future-proof my commercial EV charging installation?
Design for 2x-3x growth: add extra conduit during initial trenching, oversize the panel, and choose OCPP-compliant chargers. AMY Electric designs for scalability from the start.
