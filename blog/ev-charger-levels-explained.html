Level 1 vs Level 2 vs Level 3 EV Chargers

EV charging "levels" describe charging speed and the electrical connection required. Understanding the difference helps you choose the right setup for home and know what to expect at public stations.

📝

Level 1 (120V)

Uses a standard household outlet. Adds 3-5 miles of range per hour. Fine for low-mileage drivers but impractical as a primary home charger in car-heavy LA households.

⚡

Level 2 (240V)

The home standard. Adds 25-37 miles per hour on a 40-48 amp circuit. Requires a dedicated 240V line from your panel - exactly what we install.

🚀

Level 3 / DC Fast (480V+)

Public only. Adds 150-300 miles in 20-30 minutes using direct-current. Not installed at homes - requires industrial utility connections.

At a Glance

Which Level Is Right for You?

1
Daily LA commuter

Level 2 at home. Overnight charging covers the average 30-mile round trip with margin to spare.

2
Second car, short trips

Level 1 may suffice if you drive under 20 miles/day and can leave it plugged in constantly.

3
Long road trips

Rely on Level 3 public networks (Electrify America, EVgo) and keep Level 2 at home for daily use.

By Amperage

Charging Speed by Circuit Size

"Level 2" covers a wide range of real-world speeds. What determines your actual charging rate is the circuit amperage, and the charger is limited to 80% of the breaker rating for continuous loads.

Capacity

Panel Capacity Is the Real Constraint

The charger is the easy part. Whether you can add a 48-amp circuit depends on what the panel is already carrying. A 100-amp service feeding central AC, an electric range, a dryer, and a pool pump has little room left; adding 48 amps of continuous load pushes it past its calculated limit.

The answer is a load calculation under NEC Article 220, not a guess from the number of open breaker slots. When capacity is short, the options in order of cost are: a smaller charger circuit (24 or 32 amps), a subpanel, or a 200-amp service upgrade. A 32-amp circuit adds roughly 22 miles of range per hour, which covers almost every LA commute.

Connectors: J1772, NACS, and adapters

Level 2 charging in North America uses the J1772 connector, and Tesla vehicles ship with a J1772 adapter. Tesla's Wall Connector uses the NACS connector, and most manufacturers are now adopting NACS on new vehicles. If you are buying a charger for a car you do not own yet, a hardwired unit with a NACS connector plus a J1772 adapter covers both cases.

Home charging vs public fast charging

Home Level 2 is the cheapest electricity you will buy for the car — LADWP residential rates are a fraction of per-minute DC fast charging, and most charging happens overnight when rates are lowest. Public Level 3 exists for road trips and for households without off-street parking, not as a daily substitute.

Questions

EV Charger Levels FAQ

Can I install a Level 3 charger at home? ▾
No. DC fast chargers require 480V+ utility service and industrial infrastructure. They are only found at public and commercial stations.
Is Level 2 much faster than Level 1? ▾
Yes - about 6 to 8 times faster. Level 2 adds 25-37 miles per hour versus 3-5 for Level 1, turning an overnight charge into a few hours.
Do all EVs use the same Level 2 plug? ▾
Nearly all non-Tesla EVs in North America use the J1772 connector for Level 2. Tesla uses its own connector but includes a J1772 adapter.
How fast is a 48-amp Level 2 charger? ▾
A 48-amp charger delivers about 11 kW, adding roughly 37 miles of range per hour - enough to fully charge most EVs overnight.
What does Level 2 installation require? ▾
A dedicated 240V circuit run from your panel to the charger location, plus a permit and inspection from the city or LADWP as applicable.
How many amps do I need for Level 2 charging at home? ▾
A 40-amp circuit (32 amps delivered) is the practical minimum for most drivers and adds about 22 to 27 miles of range per hour. A 60-amp circuit feeding a 48-amp hardwired charger adds 34 to 40 miles per hour, but only if your panel's load calculation supports it. Chargers must be sized at 80% of the breaker rating for continuous load.
Can I charge an EV from a regular household outlet? ▾
Yes, that is Level 1, and it adds 3 to 5 miles of range per hour. It works for low-mileage households, but the outlet must be grounded and GFCI-protected, and it should not be shared with other loads. An extension cord is not acceptable for continuous EV charging, and a garage or carport outlet is worth adding if you plan to rely on Level 1.
Does a Level 2 charger need a permit in Los Angeles? ▾
Yes. A new 240-volt circuit requires an electrical permit and inspection, whether the charger is hardwired or plugged into a NEMA 14-50 outlet. Inside the City of Los Angeles the permit comes from LADBS; in Burbank, Glendale, Pasadena, Santa Monica, and Beverly Hills it comes from the local building department.
