03 · Cost drivers

What Drives the Cost of Non-Tesla EV Charger Installation in LA

⚡

Hardwired vs. Outlet

Hardwired chargers support higher amperage (48A) and are more permanent. NEMA 14-50 outlets are more flexible but limited to 40A continuous.

🏠

Panel Capacity

Older LA homes with 100A service may need a panel upgrade before adding a Level 2 charger. A load calculation determines this.

📏

Distance from Panel

Longer wire runs from the panel to the charging location add material and labor.

🧱

Installation Location

Garage, carport, or outdoor installation affects conduit routing and weatherproofing requirements.

🔌

Circuit Availability

If no spare 50A breaker slot exists, a subpanel or panel upgrade may be needed.

📐

Smart Features

Load management, solar integration, and smart scheduling may require additional hardware or configuration.

04 · Panel considerations

Panel Considerations for Non-Tesla EV Chargers

  • Load calculation required. A 48A charger on a 100A service may exceed available capacity. AMY Electric performs a load calculation during the site visit.
  • Panel upgrade common. Many older LA homes need a 200A panel before adding a Level 2 charger. A 100A-to-200A upgrade runs $2,500–$4,500.
  • Dedicated circuit. A Level 2 charger needs a dedicated 50A-class circuit. Sharing a circuit with other loads is not code-compliant.
  • GFCI protection. NEMA 14-50 outlets require GFCI protection per the California Electrical Code.
05 · Permits and code

Permit and Code Considerations for EV Charger Installation in LA

  • Permit required. A hardwired Level 2 charger or new NEMA 14-50 circuit requires an LADBS electrical permit and city inspection.
  • Permit fees included. LADBS permit and inspection fees ($200–$450) are included in our all-in quotes.
  • Code compliance. Installation must meet CEC requirements: dedicated circuit, proper breaker sizing, GFCI protection, and correct wire gauge.
  • LADWP rebate. Qualifying installations may be eligible for up to $1,000 in LADWP rebates. AMY Electric helps with the application.

Los Angeles uses LADBS for electrical permitting, with online filing through PermitLA and ePlanLA. Never hire a contractor who suggests skipping the permit.

07 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does non-Tesla EV charger installation cost in Los Angeles?
Non-Tesla EV charger installation in Los Angeles costs $500 to $1,200 for a hardwired Level 2 charger and $350 to $600 for a NEMA 14-50 outlet, including labor and materials. The charger unit is purchased separately by the homeowner. AMY Electric provides firm written quotes after a site visit, and the LADBS permit is included when required.
Which non-Tesla EV charger is best for home charging?
ChargePoint Home Flex, Wallbox Pulsar Plus, and JuiceBox 40 are the most popular non-Tesla home chargers in Los Angeles. All are 48A-capable, Wi-Fi connected, and UL-listed. The best choice depends on your vehicle's onboard charger limit, your panel capacity, and whether you want load management. AMY Electric installs all major brands and can help you choose.
Do I need a permit for a non-Tesla EV charger?
Yes, in most cases. A hardwired Level 2 charger or a new NEMA 14-50 circuit requires an LADBS electrical permit and a city inspection, which AMY Electric files, schedules, and attends. Permit fees are included in our all-in quotes.
Can I install a non-Tesla charger on a 100A panel?
Possibly, but a load calculation is required. A 48A charger on a 100A service may exceed available capacity, especially in homes with electric HVAC or a dryer. AMY Electric performs a load calculation during the site visit and will recommend a panel upgrade if needed. Many older LA homes need a 200A panel before adding a Level 2 charger.
Is a NEMA 14-50 outlet or hardwired charger better?
A hardwired charger is more permanent, weather-resistant, and supports higher amperage (48A). A NEMA 14-50 outlet is more flexible (you can unplug and take the charger with you) but is limited to 40A continuous and requires a GFCI breaker per code. AMY Electric installs both and can advise based on your vehicle and panel.
Does LADWP offer rebates for non-Tesla chargers?
Yes. LADWP offers rebates for qualifying Level 2 home chargers regardless of brand, typically up to $1,000 for residential installations. AMY Electric can help you navigate the rebate application. See our LADWP EV charger rebate guide for details.
