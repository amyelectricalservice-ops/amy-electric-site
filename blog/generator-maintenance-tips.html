Generator Maintenance Tips: Keep Your Backup Power Ready

A generator only helps if it starts when you need it. Whether you have a portable unit or a standby generator with a transfer switch, routine maintenance keeps it reliable through LA's fire-season outages.

🔨

Monthly

Run the generator for 15-20 minutes under load to circulate oil and charge the battery. Test the transfer switch if equipped.

💧

Oil & Filter

Change oil every 50-100 hours of run time (or annually). Use the manufacturer-specified weight - fresh oil is critical for engine life.

🔌

Battery Care

Standby units have 12V start batteries that self-discharge. Keep terminals clean and replace every 2-3 years.

🔨

Fuel Stability

Add fuel stabilizer for stored gasoline; propane and natural-gas units avoid stale-fuel issues entirely.

Annual Service

What a Pro Should Check Yearly

1
Inspect the transfer switch

Verify it isolates from utility power and engages cleanly - this is the safety-critical component.

2
Test under real load

A no-load run hides problems. We load-test to confirm the unit carries your essentials.

3
Check connections

Vibration loosens lugs and grounds over time. Torque checks prevent overheating failures.

Questions

Generator Maintenance FAQ

How often should I run my generator? ▾
At least once a month for 15-20 minutes under load. Standby units with exercise modes do this automatically.
Do I really need to change the oil if I rarely use it? ▾
Yes - annually. Even idle engines accumulate moisture and acid in the oil. An annual change protects the engine.
Why won't my generator start after sitting? ▾
Usually stale fuel, a dead start battery, or fouled spark plug. Stored gasoline degrades in 3-6 months without stabilizer.
Should a pro service my standby generator? ▾
Yes, annually. Transfer-switch testing and load verification are safety-critical and best done by a licensed electrician.
Is propane or gasoline better for backup? ▾
Propane and natural gas avoid fuel-storage problems and are ideal for whole-house standby. Gasoline is fine for portable units with stabilizer.
