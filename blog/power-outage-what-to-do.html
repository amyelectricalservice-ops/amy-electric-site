Safety Guide

What to Do During a Power Outage in Los Angeles

Outages hit LA from heat waves, Santa Ana winds, and Public Safety Power Shutoffs (PSPS). Knowing what to do keeps your family safe and your food protected until power returns.

1
Check the scope

See if neighbors are out. If just your home is dark, it may be a tripped main breaker - call us before assuming it's the utility.

2
Report it

Report the outage to LADWP (or SCE) so they can dispatch a crew. Sign up for outage alerts.

3
Unplug sensitive electronics

Power surges when service returns can damage TVs and computers. Leave one light on so you know when power is back.

4
Keep the fridge closed

A full freezer stays cold ~48 hours; a half-full one about 24. Avoid opening it unnecessarily.

Who to Call

Utility vs Electrician: Who Handles What

The most common outage mistake in LA is calling the wrong party first. Here is the split:

SymptomWho to callWhy
Whole block or neighborhood darkLADWP or SCEService territory issue, not your wiring
Just your house, meter still spinningElectricianService drop, main breaker, or panel problem
One room or circuit deadElectricianBranch circuit or breaker, not the utility
Flickering or partial power (lights dim when AC starts)ElectricianOften a loose service neutral — fire and shock risk
Downed wire in the street or yardUtility first, then electricianUtility owns the service drop to the weatherhead
Power back but electronics damagedElectricianSurge damage assessment and surge protection

If you are not sure, call us first. We can tell within a minute whether the problem is on your side of the meter, and we will route you to the utility if it is theirs.

By Duration

What to Do Based on How Long the Outage Lasts

First hour

Report it to the utility, unplug sensitive electronics, leave one light on, keep the fridge closed. Do not open windows wide in a heat event — you lose the cool air you have.

Four to eight hours

Refrigerated food is still safe. Move perishables to the freezer if there is room. Use battery-powered lighting; avoid candles. If it is hot, move to the coolest room and hydrate. Check on neighbors who may need help.

Eight to twenty-four hours

Refrigerated food should be discarded. Freezer food is still safe if the door stayed closed. If you have a generator, run it outdoors on a transfer switch only. If heat is dangerous, relocate — LA cooling centers open during major events.

Multi-day (PSPS or storm)

Treat it like an evacuation decision. A standby generator with automatic transfer is the only thing that makes a multi-day outage unremarkable. If you do not have one, the question is whether to stay or leave, not how to stretch a battery.

Prevention

Reducing Outage Impact in LA Homes

  • Whole-home surge protection at the panel. Surges at restoration are what kill electronics. A panel-level SPD is inexpensive insurance.
  • Standby generator with automatic transfer switch. Runs on natural gas, starts automatically, and powers the whole house or a critical-loads panel. Requires a permit and a licensed install.
  • Battery storage paired with solar. Quiet, no fuel, and increasingly cost-competitive. Best when paired with a critical-loads panel so the battery is not trying to run everything.
  • Medical Baseline registration. If anyone in the home depends on powered medical equipment, register with LADWP or SCE for priority restoration and rate relief.
  • Smart monitoring. A monitored panel or smart breaker tells you when power returns and what tripped, so you do not have to be home to know.

If your home loses power more often than your neighbors, that is a wiring or service problem, not a utility problem. An electrical safety inspection will tell you which.

Questions

Power Outage FAQ

Should I call the electrician or the utility first? ▾
If neighbors have power, it's likely your panel or service drop - call us. If the whole block is out, report it to LADWP/SCE first.
Is it safe to use a generator during an outage? ▾
Yes, if it's outdoors and connected through a transfer switch. Never run one indoors or backfeed a wall outlet - that risks electrocution and fires.
How long will my food stay safe? ▾
A closed freezer keeps food safe about 48 hours (24 if half full). Refrigerated food should be discarded after 4 hours without power.
Why does my power flicker during wind events? ▾
Utilities trigger PSPS events and automatic reclosers during high-wind conditions to prevent wildfire ignition. Persistent flicker at home may also signal a loose connection - worth an inspection.
Can I prevent outage disruption? ▾
A standby generator with automatic transfer, plus surge protection at the panel, dramatically reduces outage impact for LA homes.
Why does my power flicker during Santa Ana wind events? ▾
Utilities pre-emptively de-energize lines and use automatic reclosers during high-wind conditions to prevent wildfire ignition, which shows up as brief flickers or momentary outages. Persistent flicker outside of wind events, or flicker that worsens over time, is usually a loose connection at the service or panel and is worth an inspection before it becomes a failure.
Should I turn off my main breaker during an outage? ▾
No, not during a normal outage. Leave the main breaker on so power restores automatically and you know when service returns. Turn it off only if you see smoke, water, or damage at the panel, or if the utility tells you to. Turning it off and forgetting to turn it back on is a surprisingly common source of "the power is back but my house is still dark" calls.
