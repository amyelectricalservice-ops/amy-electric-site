Cost Factors

What Drives the Cost of Generator Installation in Los Angeles

  • Generator size. Whole-home standby units (14-22 kW) cost more than partial-load units, but run everything.
  • Fuel source. Natural gas is cheapest to connect where gas is available; propane needs a tank and regulator.
  • Transfer switch. An automatic transfer switch is required for standby units and adds to the scope.
  • Permits. LADBS electrical and gas permits, plus seismic anchoring on a concrete pad.
  • Site work. Concrete pad, gas line trenching, and electrical runs add cost on larger lots.

A site visit confirms the exact scope before any work is quoted.

Sizing Guide

Sizing a Generator for Your Los Angeles Home

Generators are sized by the loads you want to keep on during an outage, not by square footage. In Los Angeles the right size depends on whether you need whole-home backup or just essential circuits — and whether you plan to add an EV charger, heat pump, or future ADU to the same service. AMY Electric performs a load calculation per NEC Article 220 before recommending a size.

Backup GoalTypical SizeWhat It Runs
Essential circuits only5–8 kWFridge, lights, Wi-Fi, a few outlets. Not AC or EV charging.
Most of the house (no central AC)10–14 kWKitchen, laundry, lights, heat-pump air handler, garage doors.
Whole home including AC14–22 kWCentral AC (3–5 ton), kitchen, EV charger if load-managed. Typical LA requirement.
Large home + shop/ADU22–26 kWWhole home plus secondary panel, workshop, or detached ADU.

Undersizing is the most common regret: a 10 kW unit that cannot start a 4-ton AC compressor will trip immediately under load. Oversizing wastes fuel and money. We measure starting (inrush) amps for motors — AC compressors, well pumps, sump pumps — because starting current can be 3× running current. Homes in Sherman Oaks and Woodland Hills with longer feeder runs and hillside voltage drop need particular attention to wire sizing at higher generator outputs.

Transfer Switches & Fuel

Transfer Switches, Fuel, and Placement in Los Angeles

Never backfeed the panel without a transfer switch. Connecting a generator directly to an outlet (backfeed) energizes utility lines and can injure line workers. California Electrical Code Article 702 requires a listed transfer switch for any generator that connects to premises wiring.

Manual vs automatic transfer switches

  • Manual transfer switch ($600–$1,500 installed): You start the portable generator, plug the inlet, and flip the switch. Best for occasional PSPS outages where you are home to operate it. Interlocked breaker kits are the budget variant but provide fewer dedicated circuits.
  • Automatic transfer switch ($1,500–$3,000 installed): Detects outage in seconds, starts a standby unit on natural gas or propane, and transfers load without you present. Required for whole-home standby systems and ideal if you travel or have medical equipment.

Fuel

Natural gas is the preferred fuel where SoCalGas service exists — no refueling, indefinite run time within utility limits. Rural hillside lots without gas use propane with a dedicated tank and regulator; diesel is rare for residential due to fuel storage and AQMD considerations. Fuel choice affects pad location, gas line sizing, and LADBS permit scope.

Placement, noise, and neighbors

Standby units must sit at least 5 feet from operable windows and doors, on a level concrete pad with seismic anchoring, and with exhaust directed away from occupied space. Los Angeles noise ordinance (LAMC 112.01) and HOA rules limit nighttime decibels — modern enclosed standby units run 62–68 dB at 23 feet, while open-frame portables are significantly louder. Coastal salt air in Santa Monica and Encino hillside wind exposure both influence enclosure and anchoring choices.

Saving & Coordination

How to Control Cost and Avoid Change Orders

Generator quotes diverge most on site work and gas scope, not on the unit itself. To keep pricing transparent:

  • Get trenching, gas line, pad, and transfer switch itemized separately. Bundled quotes hide the highest-margin line items. Concrete pad should include excavation, rebar, and seismic anchoring per LADBS.
  • Run the transfer switch circuit while walls are open for other work. Pairing a generator install with a panel upgrade or whole-home rewiring shares permit and labor costs. Many LA homes still on 100A service need a 200A upgrade before a standby unit can legally be connected.
  • Ask about load management. Smart transfer switches can shed non-essential loads (EV charger, second AC zone) to stay within a smaller, cheaper generator. That often avoids a service upsizing entirely.
  • Plan for LADWP notification. Standby installs require utility notification for service coordination if solar, storage, or net metering is present; SoCalGas coordination adds a week to scheduling in some districts.

Permits & Code

Permit and Code Considerations in LA

  • Permit required. Generator installations require an LADBS electrical permit and city inspection.
  • Permit fees included. LADBS fees ($200–$450) are included in our all-in quotes.
  • Gas line permit. Standby generators require a gas line permit and inspection by LADBS or the gas utility.
  • Code compliance. Work must meet California Electrical Code, NEC Article 702, and Title 24 requirements.
  • Utility notification. LADWP must be notified for interconnection and net metering if solar is involved.

Los Angeles uses LADBS for electrical permitting. Never hire a contractor who suggests skipping the permit on generator work.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does generator installation cost in Los Angeles?
Portable generator outlets cost $500 to $1,000. Manual transfer switches cost $800 to $1,500. Automatic transfer switches cost $1,500 to $3,000. Standby generators (22kW) cost $2,500 to $7,000 installed. Costs depend on generator size, transfer switch type, and gas line requirements. AMY Electric provides firm written quotes after a site visit.
Do I need a permit for a generator installation?
Yes. Generator installations require an LADBS electrical permit and city inspection. Standby generators also require a gas line permit. AMY Electric files all permits and attends inspections. Permit fees ($200–$450) are included in all-in quotes.
What size generator do I need?
A typical LA home needs 10-22kW for whole-home backup. Smaller homes or essential circuits only need 5-10kW. AMY Electric performs a load calculation to size the right generator for your needs.
Can I run a generator during a power outage?
Yes. Standby generators auto-start within seconds of an outage. Portable generators require manual connection via a transfer switch. Never connect a generator directly to your panel without a transfer switch — it is dangerous and illegal.
How long does generator installation take?
Portable generator outlets take 2 to 4 hours. Standby generator installation takes 1 to 2 days including gas line, electrical, and inspection. LADBS inspection follows completion.
Can a standby generator power my whole house?
Yes, if it is sized for the whole load. A 14 to 22 kW standby unit runs a typical LA home including AC, EV charger, and kitchen. Smaller units (5 to 10 kW) cover essential circuits only. AMY Electric performs a load calculation to size the right unit.
Do I need to be home during generator installation?
Not for the whole project, but the electrician needs access to the panel, gas meter, and the install location. A walkthrough at the start and a handover at the end are recommended so you know how to operate and maintain the unit.
