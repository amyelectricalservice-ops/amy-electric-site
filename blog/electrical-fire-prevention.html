Electrical Fire Prevention: 10 Tips for LA Homeowners

Electrical faults are a leading cause of house fires. Most are preventable with simple habits and timely upgrades. Here are the steps Los Angeles homeowners should take.

⚠

Watch for Warning Signs

Buzzing outlets, warm switch plates, burning smells, and frequent breaker trips all signal danger. Don't ignore them.

🔌

Don't Overload Circuits

Space heaters and AC units on shared circuits cause overheating. Use dedicated circuits for high-draw appliances.

🤖

Upgrade Old Panels

FPE and Zinsco panels don't trip reliably. Federal Pacific and Zinsco panels should be replaced regardless of age.

🔦

Install AFCI/GFCI

Arc-fault (AFCI) breakers detect dangerous arcing; GFCI protects wet areas. Both are code-required in modern homes.

🔌

Check Cords & Plugs

Frayed cords and damaged plugs start fires. Replace them and avoid running cords under rugs.

📈

Label Your Panel

A clearly labeled panel lets you quickly cut power in an emergency. We offer panel labeling service.

High Risk

Homes That Need Immediate Attention

1
Pre-1970s wiring

Knob-and-tube and cloth-covered wiring lack grounding and pose elevated fire risk.

2
Aluminum branch wiring

Common in 1960s-70s homes, aluminum expands and loosens at connections - a known fire hazard unless properly terminated.

3
Unaltered older panels

If your panel is original to a 50-year-old home, have it evaluated for capacity and safety.

Questions

Electrical Fire Prevention FAQ

What are the first signs of an electrical fire risk? ▾
Burning smells near outlets, discolored or warm switch plates, buzzing sounds, and breakers that trip repeatedly are the earliest warnings.
Do AFCI breakers really prevent fires? ▾
Yes. AFCIs detect arc faults - the high-temperature sparks that start many electrical fires - and shut the circuit off before ignition.
Are Federal Pacific panels dangerous? ▾
FPE (Stab-Lok) panels are widely documented to fail to trip under fault conditions. Most inspectors recommend replacement.
Can I prevent fires with surge protection? ▾
Whole-home surge protection reduces damage from voltage spikes, but it's not a substitute for AFCI/GFCI and proper wiring.
How often should I get an electrical inspection? ▾
Every 5-10 years for newer homes, and immediately if you buy a home over 30 years old or notice any warning signs.
