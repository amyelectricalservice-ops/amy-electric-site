Cost Factors

What Drives the Cost of an Electrical Safety Inspection in Los Angeles

  • Home size. Larger homes take longer to inspect.
  • Panel complexity. Older panels with recalled breakers need closer examination.
  • Accessibility. Attic and crawlspace access affects inspection time.
  • Report depth. A full written report with photos and code references costs more than a visual check.
  • Follow-up. Inspection plus repair bundling can reduce total cost.

A site visit confirms the exact scope before any work is quoted.

03 · When you need one

When You Need an Electrical Safety Inspection

  • Buying or selling a home. Inspectors flag electrical issues; a licensed report prevents escrow surprises.
  • Insurance requirement. Many California carriers require an inspection before issuing or renewing a policy, especially for older homes.
  • Remodel or addition. Code requires updated protection when walls are opened.
  • Older home. Homes built before 1970 may have knob-and-tube, aluminum, or ungrounded wiring.
  • Recurring problems. Tripping breakers, flickering lights, or burning smells warrant an inspection.
04 · Insurance and real estate

How an Inspection Helps with Insurance and Real Estate

  • Insurance. A licensed inspection report satisfies carrier requirements and can restore standard coverage and rates.
  • Escrow. Buyers and sellers use the report to negotiate repairs or credits before closing.
  • Documentation. AMY Electric provides the permit and inspection record that insurers and escrow officers request.
  • Peace of mind. Identifies hazards before they become fires or outages.
05 · Permits and code

Permit and Code Considerations for Inspections in LA

  • No permit for inspection alone. An inspection does not require a permit.
  • Permit for repairs. If repairs are made, a permit may be required depending on scope.
  • Code references. Inspections reference LADBS, California Electrical Code, and Title 24 requirements.
  • Permit fees included. When AMY performs repairs, LADBS fees ($200–$450) are included in all-in quotes.

Los Angeles uses LADBS for electrical permitting. Never hire a contractor who suggests skipping a permit on repair work.

06 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an electrical safety inspection cost in Los Angeles?
A standard home electrical safety inspection in Los Angeles costs $150 to $400. A full pre-purchase or insurance inspection with a written report runs $300 to $800. The price depends on home size, panel count, and whether a written report is required. AMY Electric provides firm written quotes before the visit.
What is included in an electrical safety inspection?
An inspection covers the service panel, wiring type and condition, grounding and bonding, GFCI and AFCI protection, smoke and CO detector placement, and visible code violations. AMY Electric provides a written summary of findings and recommended repairs.
When do I need an electrical safety inspection?
Common triggers include buying or selling a home, an insurance requirement, a remodel or addition, an older home with original wiring, recurring breaker trips, or a burning smell. Inspectors and insurers frequently require a licensed electrician's report.
Will an inspection help with insurance?
Yes. Many California insurers require a licensed electrician's inspection report before issuing or renewing a policy, especially for older homes with knob-and-tube or aluminum wiring. AMY Electric provides the documentation insurers request.
Is an inspection the same as a repair?
No. An inspection identifies issues; repairs are quoted separately. If repairs are needed, AMY Electric provides a written estimate and can schedule the work. Diagnostic findings are credited toward the repair when booked together.
Do I need a permit for an inspection?
No permit is required for an inspection alone. If repairs are made, a permit may be required depending on scope. AMY Electric files any required LADBS permits and attends the inspection.
How long does an electrical safety inspection take?
60 to 120 minutes for a typical home; longer for larger homes or those with complex systems. The written report follows within 24 to 48 hours.
Should I get an inspection before buying a home?
Yes. A pre-purchase electrical inspection identifies hidden issues that a general home inspector may miss, especially in older LA homes with aluminum or knob-and-tube wiring.
