Zinsco Panels

What Is a Zinsco Panel and Why Is It Dangerous?

Zinsco (also sold under the Sylvania brand) panels were installed in millions of homes from the 1950s through the 1970s. They are now considered one of the most hazardous electrical panels in residential use due to documented breaker failure rates.

The Hazard

Why Zinsco Panels Are a Fire Risk

Zinsco panels have a well-documented design flaw: the breakers can fail to trip during an overcurrent condition. This means the breaker may not shut off power when it should, allowing wires to overheat and potentially start a fire.

Key hazards

  • Breaker failure to trip. The most dangerous flaw. Under fault conditions, Zinsco breakers may not disconnect, allowing sustained overcurrent.
  • Bus bar corrosion. The aluminum bus bars corrode over time, creating high-resistance connections that generate heat.
  • Breaker-to-bus connection loss. Breakers can lose contact with the bus bar, creating arcing and overheating.
  • No replacement parts. Zinsco went out of business. Replacement breakers are no longer manufactured; only aftermarket parts exist, and they do not fix the fundamental design flaw.

Los Angeles homes built between 1950 and 1975 are the most likely to have Zinsco panels. If your panel says "Zinsco" or "Sylvania" on the label, it should be replaced.

Insurance

Insurance Requirements for Zinsco Panels

Most California insurance companies refuse to cover homes with Zinsco panels, or charge significantly higher premiums. Common insurance requirements include:

  • Policy denial or non-renewal. Many carriers will not write or renew a policy on a home with a Zinsco panel.
  • Higher premiums. Some carriers offer coverage at 2-3x the standard rate.
  • Required replacement. Some carriers require replacement as a condition of coverage, with a deadline (often 30-90 days).
  • Documentation. Insurers typically require a permit and inspection record proving the panel was replaced by a licensed electrician.

Replacement Process

What to Expect During Zinsco Panel Replacement

  1. Site visit and assessment. We inspect the panel, assess the service entrance, and provide a written estimate.
  2. Permit filing. We file the LADBS electrical permit before work begins.
  3. Utility coordination. If the service entrance needs upgrading, we coordinate with LADWP or SCE.
  4. Panel replacement. We remove the Zinsco panel, install a new 200A panel, and reconnect all circuits. Power is off for 4 to 8 hours.

FAQ

Zinsco Panel Replacement FAQ

How do I know if I have a Zinsco panel? ▾
Check the label on your electrical panel. If it says "Zinsco" or "Sylvania" anywhere on the panel or breakers, you have a Zinsco panel. Los Angeles homes built between 1950 and 1975 are the most likely to have them. An electrician can confirm during an inspection.
Are Zinsco panels dangerous? ▾
Yes. Zinsco panels have documented breaker failure-to-trip rates. Under fault conditions, the breakers may not disconnect, allowing wires to overheat and potentially start a fire. The bus bars also corrode over time, creating additional hazards. Replacement is the uniform professional recommendation.
Can I replace just the breakers instead of the whole panel? ▾
No. Replacement breakers do not fix the fundamental design flaw — the bus bar and connection design remain defective. The entire panel must be replaced to eliminate the hazard. AMY Electric replaces the full panel with a modern 200A unit.
Will my insurance require Zinsco panel replacement? ▾
Most California insurers refuse to cover homes with Zinsco panels, or charge significantly higher premiums. Some require replacement as a condition of coverage with a deadline. AMY Electric provides the permit and inspection records insurers request.
How long does Zinsco panel replacement take? ▾
The physical swap takes 4 to 8 hours with the power off. LADBS inspection follows within 48 hours. If LADWP or SCE must upgrade the service connection, add 1 to 3 weeks. Most projects finish within 1 to 3 weeks.
Is Zinsco the same as Sylvania? ▾
Zinsco panels were also sold under the Sylvania brand. If your panel says "Sylvania" on the label, it is likely a Zinsco panel with the same hazards. Both require replacement.
