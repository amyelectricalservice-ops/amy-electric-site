Why Panel Upgrades Matter

Why Panel Upgrades Are Critical for LA Homes

Many Los Angeles homes, particularly those built before the 1980s, still operate with outdated 60A or 100A electrical panels. As our electrical demands have grown with air conditioning, home offices, EV charging, and modern appliances, these older panels can become safety hazards and fail to meet current needs. Here are the key warning signs that indicate it's time for a panel upgrade.

⚡

Frequent Breaker Trips

If your breakers trip regularly when using multiple appliances or running your AC, it's a clear sign your panel is overloaded and needs upgrading to handle your home's electrical demand.

💡

Flickering or Dimming Lights

Lights that flicker or dim when appliances turn on (especially large ones like AC units or refrigerators) indicate voltage fluctuations from an inadequate panel.

🔌

Insufficient Outlets

Relying heavily on power strips and extension cords because you don't have enough outlets often means your electrical system—and possibly your panel—is outdated.

🏠

Older Home (Pre-1980)

Homes built before 1980 often have 60A or 100A panels that weren't designed for today's electrical loads. If you haven't upgraded your panel, it's likely time for an update.

🔋

Planning Major Additions

Adding a home office, EV charger, workshop, or major renovation? These additions significantly increase your electrical load and may require a panel upgrade to support them safely.

⚠️

Warm or Discolored Panel

If your electrical panel feels warm to the touch, shows discoloration, or has a burning smell, these are serious safety hazards requiring immediate attention from a licensed electrician.

LA-Specific Considerations

Los Angeles-Specific Panel Upgrade Factors

☀️

Air Conditioning Demands

LA's climate necessitates reliable AC operation. Older panels often struggle to handle the startup surge of central air conditioning units, causing breaker trips and potential damage.

🚗

EV Charger Preparation

As EV adoption accelerates in Los Angeles, many homeowners are finding their panels can't support the additional 40-50 amp circuit needed for a Level 2 EV charger without an upgrade.

🏢

Home Office & Remote Work

The rise of remote work has increased electrical demands in LA homes with multiple computers, monitors, printers, and networking equipment running simultaneously throughout the day.

📋

LADWP Requirements

Los Angeles Department of Water and Power may require a panel upgrade before approving certain electrical work or service increases, particularly for EV charger installations or major renovations.

Quick checklist

Top warning signs your panel needs an upgrade

Use this quick checklist to spot the most common symptoms. If two or more apply, schedule a licensed inspection.

1. Breakers trip often

If your breaker trips whenever the AC, microwave, dryer, or EV charger turns on, your panel may be overloaded or losing capacity. Frequent tripping is one of the clearest warning signs.

2. Lights flicker or dim

Lights that dim when large appliances start are a sign the panel is struggling with voltage drops. This often shows up in older houses with undersized service.

3. The panel feels hot or smells unusual

If the panel is warm to the touch, shows discoloration, or gives off a burning odor, stop using it and schedule an inspection immediately. Those symptoms can signal a serious fire risk.

4. There are scorch marks or damaged breakers

Burn marks around breaker handles or arcing signs are not normal. They suggest the panel is aging or has loose connections and should be inspected by a licensed electrician.

5. You have an old panel

Panels more than 25-40 years old should be evaluated. Some older brands are especially risky and may need replacement even if they still work.

6. You are adding major electrical loads

If you are installing a new EV charger, adding an ADU, upgrading the HVAC, or remodeling the kitchen, your electrical demand may exceed what your current panel can safely support.

7. You rely on extension cords and power strips

If your home has too few dedicated outlets and you depend on plug strips to handle everyday demands, the electrical system may be stretched beyond its intended capacity.

8. You have outdated or unsupported brands

Older electrical systems, some fuse panels, or legacy breaker brands may not meet today’s safety standards. Replacement is often the safer and smarter option.

Schedule an inspection if your breaker box is warm, if breakers are failing, if you are adding high-demand equipment, or if you still have an older panel that may no longer be code-compliant. The goal is to prevent safety problems rather than react after a failure.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About Panel Upgrade Warning Signs

What are the warning signs I need a panel upgrade? ▾
The most common warning signs include frequent breaker trips, flickering or dimming lights when appliances turn on, a warm or discolored panel, two-prong outlets throughout the home, a panel over 40 years old (especially Federal Pacific or Zinsco brands), and planning to add major loads like an EV charger or AC unit.
How old is too old for an electrical panel? ▾
Electrical panels over 40 years old should be inspected by a licensed electrician. Panels manufactured before 1990, particularly Federal Pacific and Zinsco brands, are known safety hazards with documented failure rates. Even panels that appear functional may have internal corrosion or connection issues that increase fire risk.
Can I add an EV charger without upgrading my panel? ▾
It depends on your current panel capacity and available breaker spaces. A Level 2 EV charger typically requires a 50A dedicated circuit. If your panel is already near capacity or lacks open breaker slots, an upgrade is necessary. A licensed electrician can perform a load calculation to determine if your existing panel can support the additional load.
