Smart Controls & Cost Saving

Smart Controls, Timers, and Energy Costs in Los Angeles

Fixture cost is only half the lifetime cost of outdoor lighting — the other half is energy, lamp life, and controls. Los Angeles homeowners who plan controls during the initial install spend less over ten years than those who add them later.

Controls that pay for themselves

  • Astronomical timer ($80–$180): Turns lights on at sunset and off at sunrise, adjusting daily for LA latitude. More accurate than a fixed timer and cheaper than a photocell that drifts with dust and weather.
  • Smart transformer / Wi-Fi switch ($150–$350): App and voice control via Alexa, Google, or Lutron. Zone your yard — path lights, security floods, and patio strings on separate schedules — so you only run what you need.
  • Motion + photocell combo ($60–$140 per fixture): Security floods stay off until motion after dark, cutting energy use by 80–90% versus dusk-to-dawn.

LED vs solar vs line-voltage operating cost

Quality LED path lights (3–5W each) cost roughly $2–$4 per year per fixture to operate on LADWP rates. Solar fixtures have zero operating cost but need panel cleaning, battery replacement every 2–3 years, and deliver lower, inconsistent output — unsuitable for security. Low-voltage LED remains the sweet spot: low operating cost, long life (25,000–50,000 hours), and no permit for the landscape portion.

Bulletproofing for LA conditions

Specify marine-grade brass or copper fixtures within 5 miles of the coast (Santa Monica, Marina del Rey) and UV-stable composite elsewhere. Conduit and transformer enclosures should be rated for 110°F+ attic and enclosure temperatures — Valley summers in Woodland Hills and Burbank regularly exceed ratings of import fixtures. Design & Layers

Designing Layers: A Professional Approach

Professionals design outdoor lighting in layers, not as a list of fixtures. Each layer has a purpose, a circuit, and a control.

LayerPurposeTypical Fixture
WayfindingSafe path from street to doorLow-voltage path lights, step lights
SecurityDeter intrusion, aid camerasMotion floods, wall sconces with photocell
AccentHighlight architecture and treesUplights, well lights, hardscape lights
Task / livingMake patios usable after darkString lights, dimmable pendants, step lights

A good design conversation starts with how you use the yard at night, not with a catalog. Walk the property after dark with the electrician, note dark corners, glare from existing porch lights, and where cameras need even illumination. Outdoor lighting is the one trade where aesthetics and code intersect most visibly — fixture placement affects both safety and inspection.

Layering matters electrically because wayfinding and accent run on low-voltage landscape circuits (12V, permit-exempt), while security floods and patio receptacles are line-voltage (120V) and require GFCI, permits, and often a new homerun to the panel. Designing them together during one trenching and wiring phase saves 20–30% versus adding layers later. We also pre-wire for future cameras and speakers while the trench is open.

LA Considerations

Los Angeles Climate and Grid Considerations for Outdoor Lighting

LA's climate and grid create specific requirements for outdoor electrical work that generic guides miss:

  • UV and heat. Fixtures and conduit must be UV-stable and rated for high ambient temperatures; coastal salt air requires marine-grade finishes.
  • Santa Ana winds. Secure mounting and weatherproof enclosures prevent wind damage and moisture intrusion.
  • PSPS events. Public Safety Power Shutoffs can leave outdoor lighting dark; battery-backed or solar-assisted fixtures maintain safety during outages.
  • Water restrictions. Smart irrigation controllers and low-voltage landscape lighting reduce water and energy use.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About Outdoor Lighting

How much does outdoor lighting installation cost in Los Angeles? ▾
Basic landscape lighting starts around $200 to $500 for a few path lights, while a comprehensive outdoor lighting system with landscape, security, and patio lighting can cost $1,000 to $5,000 or more depending on the number of fixtures, wiring complexity, and whether you want smart controls or automation.
Do I need a permit for outdoor lighting in LA? ▾
Low-voltage landscape lighting (12V) typically does not require a permit in Los Angeles. However, line-voltage (120V) outdoor lighting, new circuits, or any work that modifies your electrical system requires a permit from LADBS. If you are adding new outdoor outlets or hardwired fixtures, a permit and inspection are required.
Can outdoor lighting be solar-powered? ▾
Solar-powered outdoor lighting is available and requires no wiring, making it a good option for renters or temporary setups. However, solar fixtures are generally less bright, less reliable on cloudy days, and shorter-lived than hardwired LED systems. For consistent, code-compliant outdoor lighting, hardwired fixtures are the preferred choice.
How much does a full backyard outdoor lighting system cost in Los Angeles? ▾
A comprehensive exterior system covering landscape, security, and patio lighting typically runs $1,500 to $6,000 in Los Angeles, including fixtures, transformers, trenching, and installation. Whole-house approaches that add dedicated circuits, smart controls, or automation can exceed that. Low-voltage landscape lighting is generally permit-exempt, while line-voltage work is not.
What happens to outdoor lighting during a power outage? ▾
Hardwired outdoor fixtures follow your home's circuit, so they shut off during a grid outage. Solar fixtures keep working. For lighting that should stay on during outages, add battery backup or transfer-switch-protected circuits during the install.
How do I protect outdoor lighting from LA's heat and salt air? ▾
Use UV-stable, weatherproof (wet-rated) fixtures and marine-grade finishes in coastal areas. AMY Electric selects fixtures rated for LA's climate and secures them against Santa Ana winds.
Can outdoor lighting be automated with smart controls? ▾
Yes. Astronomical timers, motion sensors, and app control are common. AMY Electric installs smart transformers and switches compatible with Alexa, Google, and Lutron.
