ADU Electrical Panel Requirements in Los Angeles

Planning an Accessory Dwelling Unit (ADU) in LA? Here is what you need to know about main panel sizing, 100A subpanels, dedicated LADWP meters, and LADBS permit compliance.

Main Panel Capacity: 100A vs 200A Upgrades

Before adding an ADU, your electrician must perform a load calculation per NEC Article 220. Most older homes in Los Angeles have a 100-amp main breaker. Adding an ADU with electric HVAC, cooking, and water heating usually requires upgrading your main electrical panel to 200 amps or 400 amps.

Subpanel vs. Dedicated LADWP Utility Meter

When wiring an ADU, property owners have two primary options:

  • 100A Subpanel (Fed from Main House): Most cost-effective option. Powers the ADU directly from the primary electrical panel.
  • Dedicated Utility Meter (Dual Socket): Best for rental ADUs. Requires installing a dual-meter socket adapter and coordinating directly with LADWP or Southern California Edison (SCE).

2026 California Electrical Code & LADBS Permits

All ADU electrical work in Los Angeles requires permits from the Department of Building and Safety (LADBS). Key 2026 CEC requirements include:

  • Dedicated AFCI and GFCI circuit breaker protection throughout the unit.
  • Underground rigid conduit running from main house to detached garage/ADU.
  • Title 24 solar-ready electrical infrastructure.

What Electrical Scope an LA ADU Actually Needs

Panel size is only half the decision. The circuits inside the unit determine the load you are adding. A typical Los Angeles ADU includes:

  • Kitchen: Dedicated 20A small-appliance circuits, a dedicated circuit for the range or cooktop, and GFCI protection at counter receptacles.
  • HVAC: A dedicated 240V circuit for a mini-split or heat pump. This is usually the largest single load in the unit.
  • Water heating: A dedicated 240V circuit for a tank or tankless electric water heater, or a gas line if the unit is set up for gas.
  • Laundry: A dedicated 240V dryer circuit and a 20A washer circuit where laundry is included.
  • Lighting and receptacles: AFCI-protected bedroom and living circuits, plus Title 24-compliant lighting controls.
  • Smoke and CO: Interconnected smoke and carbon monoxide alarms tied to the unit's power supply.
  • EV-ready: A dedicated 240V circuit or capped conduit run so a future charger does not require opening walls.

Service Upgrade or Load Management?

Not every ADU requires a 200A or 400A service upgrade. A load calculation per NEC Article 220 tells you whether the existing service can carry the added load. When the numbers are close, a smart panel or load-management device can share capacity between the main house and the ADU instead of replacing the service. That decision belongs in the design phase — before trenching and rough-in — because it changes the conduit, panel, and meter plan.

ADU Electrical FAQ

Does an ADU need its own electrical panel?
Not always. A 100A subpanel fed from the main house is the most cost-effective option for guest suites, home offices, and pool houses. A dedicated utility meter makes more sense for a long-term rental where the tenant pays their own bill.
Will adding an ADU require a main panel upgrade?
It depends on the existing service size and the appliances in the unit. Many older LA homes have 100A service, and an ADU with electric HVAC, cooking, and water heating usually pushes the load past that. A load calculation per NEC Article 220 confirms whether a 200A upgrade is required.
How much does ADU electrical work cost in Los Angeles?
A 100A subpanel from the main house runs $1,800 to $3,500. A dedicated utility meter runs $4,500 to $7,500. A 200A main panel upgrade runs $2,500 to $4,500. Underground trenching and conduit runs $25 to $50 per linear foot. Final pricing depends on distance from the main panel and the appliances in the unit.
Do I need a permit for ADU electrical work?
Yes. All ADU electrical work in Los Angeles requires a permit from the Department of Building and Safety (LADBS) and inspection before the unit is energized. AMY Electric files the permit and attends the inspection.
Can I add an EV charger to an ADU?
Yes. We size the ADU panel with capacity for a future 240V charger, or install a dedicated circuit now. Running the conduit during construction is far cheaper than adding it later.
