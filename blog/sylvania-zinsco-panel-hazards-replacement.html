Sylvania & Zinsco Electrical Panel Replacement Guide

Many Los Angeles homeowners look at their electrical panel and see a badge reading "SYLVANIA". Learn why Sylvania panels are identical to Zinsco panels, the severe fire hazards they pose, and how AMY Electric replaces them safely.

01 · History & Brand Connection

Is a Sylvania Electrical Panel the Same as a Zinsco Panel?

Yes. A common source of confusion for Los Angeles homeowners is the brand name on their panel. In 1973, GTE Sylvania purchased Zinsco Electrical Products. GTE Sylvania continued to manufacture and market the exact same panel enclosure, bus bar architecture, and breaker design under the Sylvania brand name throughout the 1970s and 1980s.

If your home was built or remodeled in Los Angeles during the 1970s or 1980s, you likely have a Sylvania main service panel or subpanel. Although it carries the Sylvania nameplate, internally it is a Zinsco design and suffers from the exact same documented hazards.

02 · Major Safety Hazards

The Specific Failure Mechanisms of Sylvania Zinsco Panels

Sylvania Zinsco panels have three primary design flaws that make them a major electrical safety hazard:

1. Aluminum Bus Bar Corrosion & Pitting

Zinsco and Sylvania panels utilized aluminum bus bars. Over decades of operation, the connection points between the breakers and the aluminum bar oxidize, pit, and corrode. Corrosion increases electrical resistance, producing extreme localized heat.

2. Breakers Welding directly to the Bus Bar

As the copper clips on the back of the breaker heat up on the pitted aluminum bus bar, they can melt and weld directly onto the bus bar itself. Once welded, the breaker can no longer trip mechanically during an overload or short circuit. Heavy current flows unchecked through house wiring, leading to electrical fires inside walls.

3. False Sense of Security

Even if a breaker handle appears to be in the "ON" position and everything seems to work normally, the internal mechanism may already be fused. You cannot tell if a Sylvania breaker will trip simply by looking at it.

03 · Home Insurance & Escrow

Home Insurance & Real Estate Transaction Impacts

Because the fire risks of Sylvania and Zinsco panels are well-documented across the insurance industry:

  • Policy Denials: Insurance providers in California frequently refuse to write new homeowner policies on houses with Sylvania or Zinsco panels.
  • Escrow Demands: Home inspectors instantly flag Sylvania panels on inspection reports. Buyers often demand a full panel replacement credit or complete replacement prior to closing escrow.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About Sylvania and Zinsco Panels

Is a Sylvania Electrical Panel the Same as a Zinsco Panel? ▾
Yes. GTE Sylvania acquired Zinsco Electrical Products in 1973 and continued manufacturing the identical panel line under the Sylvania and GTE-Sylvania brands through the 1970s and 1980s. Same aluminum bus bars, same breaker-to-bus clip design, same documented failure modes; only the badge changed. Confirm yours by the door badge reading SYLVANIA or GTE Sylvania and the interior's colorful breaker handles over aluminum bus work. If your panel matches, treat every question here as applying to your home.
How much does Sylvania or Zinsco panel replacement cost in Los Angeles? ▾
A standard 100A to 200A panel upgrade runs $2,500 to $4,500, covering the permit, labor, materials, and inspection. When the old equipment is a Zinsco or Federal Pacific panel, published replacement pricing with remediation runs $2,800 to $4,800. LADBS permit and inspection fees, $200 to $450, are included in our quotes. The utility service connection, typically $300 to $600, bills separately through LADWP or SCE.
How long does a Sylvania or Zinsco panel replacement take? ▾
The physical replacement takes four to eight hours with the power off. LADBS inspection typically follows within 48 hours of completion. If LADWP or SCE must upgrade your service connection for the higher amperage, add one to three weeks of utility-side time that runs parallel to our work. From signed estimate to passed inspection, most straightforward replacements finish within one to three weeks.
Do I need a permit to replace a Sylvania or Zinsco panel in Los Angeles? ▾
Yes. Replacing a panel requires an LADBS electrical permit, and a city inspector verifies the work against the California Electrical Code before the job is closed. LADBS runs its permitting online through PermitLA and ePlanLA, and AMY Electric files the permit, schedules the inspection, and attends it for you. Unpermitted panel work resurfaces at the sale of the home, after insurance claims, and the next time the panel is opened.
Will my insurance cover the cost of replacing a Zinsco panel? ▾
Your carrier decides, and it is worth asking them before you rely on any answer. Insurers in California frequently decline to write new homeowner policies on houses with documented-hazard equipment, which is why replacing a Zinsco or Federal Pacific panel usually comes up during escrow rather than after a claim. The replacement cost itself is generally the owner's or buyer's to carry, and what a policy covers once the work is done depends on your policy terms.
