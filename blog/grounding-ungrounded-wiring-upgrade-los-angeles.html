02 · Hazards

Why Ungrounded Wiring Is a Hazard

  • No fault path. Without a ground, fault current has no safe path to trip a breaker, increasing shock risk.
  • Appliance risk. Modern appliances with three-prong plugs expect a ground; without it, metal cases can become energized.
  • Insurance. Many insurers require grounding upgrades or GFCI protection for coverage.
  • Code. Current code requires grounding for new circuits and GFCI protection where grounding is not feasible.
03 · GFCI alternative

GFCI Protection as an Alternative to Full Grounding

  • When full rewire is not feasible. GFCI protection on ungrounded circuits provides shock protection without rewiring.
  • Labeled outlets. GFCI-protected ungrounded outlets must be labeled "No Equipment Ground."
  • Limitations. GFCI does not create a true ground; surge protectors and some electronics still need a ground.
  • Best practice. Full grounding is preferred; GFCI is the code-accepted alternative when rewiring is impractical.
04 · Permits and code

Permit and Code Considerations in LA

  • Permit required. A grounding upgrade or rewiring requires an LADBS electrical permit and city inspection.
  • Permit fees included. LADBS fees ($200–$450) are included in our all-in quotes.
  • Code compliance. Work must meet current California Electrical Code requirements.

Los Angeles uses LADBS for electrical permitting. Never hire a contractor who suggests skipping the permit on grounding work.

What is ungrounded wiring?
Ungrounded wiring lacks a ground wire, common in homes built before 1960. Without a ground, fault current has no safe path, increasing shock and fire risk. Older two-prong outlets are a sign of ungrounded wiring.
Can I use GFCI instead of grounding?
Yes, in some cases. GFCI protection can be installed on ungrounded circuits as an alternative when full rewiring is not feasible. This provides shock protection but does not create a true ground. AMY Electric advises on the safest option for your home.
Is ungrounded wiring dangerous?
Ungrounded wiring increases shock and fire risk, especially with modern appliances that require a ground. Many insurers require grounding upgrades or GFCI protection for coverage.
