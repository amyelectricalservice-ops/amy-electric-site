02 · Hazards

Why Cloth-Insulated Wiring Is a Hazard

  • Brittle insulation. Rubber and cloth insulation degrades and cracks, exposing live conductors.
  • Fire risk. Exposed conductors can arc and ignite surrounding material.
  • Shock risk. Cracked insulation exposes live wires to touch.
  • Overloaded circuits. Cloth wiring was designed for far lower loads than modern homes require.
  • No ground. Cloth wiring lacks a ground wire, so GFCI/AFCI protection is limited.
03 · Insurance

Insurance and Cloth-Insulated Wiring

  • Refusal to insure. Many California insurers refuse to write or renew policies on homes with cloth-insulated wiring.
  • Higher premiums. Homes with cloth wiring may face higher premiums or coverage restrictions.
  • Restored coverage. Replacing the wiring can restore standard coverage and rates.
  • Documentation. AMY Electric provides the permit and inspection record insurers request.
04 · Permits and code

Permit and Code Considerations in LA

  • Permit required. A whole-home or partial rewiring requires an LADBS electrical permit and city inspection.
  • Permit fees included. LADBS fees ($200–$450) are included in our all-in quotes.
  • Code compliance. New wiring must meet current California Electrical Code, including grounding and GFCI/AFCI protection.

Los Angeles uses LADBS for electrical permitting. Never hire a contractor who suggests skipping the permit on a rewire.
