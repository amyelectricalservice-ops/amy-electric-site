Does Homeowners Insurance Require a Panel Upgrade in Los Angeles?

Many Los Angeles homeowners are surprised to learn that their insurance company may require an electrical panel upgrade — or refuse to renew their policy — based on the type and age of their electrical panel. This guide explains the LA County cooling mandate, insurance requirements, and what licensed electricians recommend.

LA County Mandate

LA County 82°F Cooling Mandate and Panel Upgrades

Los Angeles County has specific requirements for residential cooling and electrical capacity. The 82°F cooling mandate and related electrical code requirements can trigger panel upgrade needs when:

  • Adding central air conditioning to a home that was not originally equipped with it.
  • Upgrading from a 100A to 200A service to support modern loads like EV chargers, heat pumps, and induction cooking.
  • Replacing an outdated panel (Zinsco, Federal Pacific, Pushmatic) that does not meet current code.
  • Adding a subpanel for an ADU, garage conversion, or home addition.

The mandate does not automatically require a panel upgrade for every home, but it does require that the electrical system can safely support the cooling equipment. If your panel is at capacity or uses outdated technology, an upgrade is typically needed.

Insurance Requirements

Insurance Requirements for Panel Upgrades in Los Angeles

Insurance companies increasingly require electrical panel upgrades or inspections before binding or renewing coverage. Common triggers include:

  • Zinsco, Federal Pacific, or Pushmatic panels. Many carriers refuse to cover homes with these panels due to documented failure-to-trip rates.
  • Aluminum branch wiring. Homes built 1965-1978 with aluminum wiring may face higher premiums or coverage denial.
  • Knob-and-tube wiring. Pre-1950s homes with K&T wiring are often uninsurable at standard rates.
  • Fuse boxes. Old fuse-based panels are frequently flagged during insurance inspections.
  • Overloaded or undersized panels. A 60A or 100A panel serving a modern home with high loads may trigger a requirement.

If your insurance company requires a panel upgrade, AMY Electric provides the inspection record and permit documentation they need. We coordinate with your insurer to ensure the work meets their requirements.

What to Do

What to Do If Your Insurance Requires a Panel Upgrade

  1. Get a professional inspection. A licensed C-10 electrician can assess your panel and provide a written report with photos.
  2. Get a written quote. AMY Electric provides firm written quotes for panel upgrades, including permit and inspection costs.
  3. Schedule the work. Most panel upgrades take 4 to 8 hours with the power off. LADBS inspection follows within 48 hours.
  4. Provide documentation. AMY Electric provides the permit record, inspection certificate, and photos your insurer needs.

A 100A to 200A panel upgrade typically runs $2,500 to $4,500 in Los Angeles, including the panel, labor, permit, and inspection. If the service entrance or meter must also be upgraded, add $1,000 to $3,000.

Permits & Code

Permit and Code Considerations in LA

  • Permit required. Panel upgrades require an LADBS electrical permit and city inspection.
  • Permit fees included. LADBS fees ($200-$450) are included in our all-in quotes.
  • Utility coordination. LADWP or SCE may need to upgrade the service connection for higher amperage.
  • Code compliance. Work must meet California Electrical Code and NEC requirements.

Los Angeles uses LADBS for electrical permitting. Never hire a contractor who suggests skipping a permit on panel work.
