Commercial Electrical

Understanding Commercial Electrical Work in LA

Commercial electrical projects in Los Angeles involve different requirements, codes, and considerations than residential work. Whether you're planning a tenant improvement, LED retrofit, or EV fleet charging installation, understanding the commercial electrical landscape is essential for business owners.

🏢

Tenant Improvements

Office buildouts, retail spaces, and restaurant renovations require specialized electrical planning, higher amperage service, and commercial-grade equipment and materials.

💡

Commercial LED Retrofits

Upgrading to LED lighting can reduce energy costs by 50-70%. Many LA businesses qualify for utility rebates and tax incentives for commercial lighting upgrades.

🚗

EV Fleet Charging

As businesses electrify their fleets, commercial-grade EV charging stations require higher capacity service, load management, and specialized installation expertise.

🔧

Panel & Service Upgrades

Growing businesses often need 400A or higher service to support increased electrical demands. Commercial service upgrades require coordination with LADWP or SCE.

⚡

Emergency & Standby Power

Backup generators and UPS systems keep critical operations running during power outages—essential for data centers, medical facilities, and 24/7 businesses.

🕐

After-Hours Service

Commercial electrical work often needs to be performed outside business hours to minimize disruption. Many LA electricians offer overnight and weekend service.

Permits & Compliance

LA Commercial Electrical Permits & Codes

📋

LADBS Permits Required

All commercial electrical work in LA requires permits from the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety. We handle all permit applications and inspection scheduling.

🏗️

Commercial Building Codes

Commercial projects must comply with the California Electrical Code, Title 24 energy requirements, ADA accessibility, and local LA amendments.

⚖️

Title 24 Compliance

California's Title 24 energy standards apply to commercial lighting, HVAC controls, and electrical systems. Non-compliance can result in failed inspections and penalties.

🔍

Inspection Process

Commercial electrical work typically requires multiple inspections: rough-in, service, and final. We schedule and attend all required inspections.

Choosing a Contractor

How to Choose a Commercial Electrician in LA

1
Verify Licensing & Insurance

Ensure your electrician holds a valid California C-10 license, general liability insurance, and workers' compensation coverage. Request proof before work begins.

2
Check Commercial Experience

Commercial work differs significantly from residential. Look for electricians with documented experience in similar commercial projects and industries.

3
Review References & Portfolio

Ask for references from similar commercial projects and review their portfolio. Established LA commercial electricians will have a track record you can verify.

4
Get Detailed Written Estimates

Professional commercial electricians provide detailed written estimates including scope, materials, labor, permit costs, and timeline. Avoid verbal estimates for commercial work.

5
Confirm Permit Handling

Your electrician should handle all permitting and inspection coordination. Never hire a contractor who suggests skipping permits to save money.
