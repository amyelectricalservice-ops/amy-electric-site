01 · What knob and tube is

What Knob and Tube Wiring Is and Why LA Homes Still Have It

Knob and tube (K&T) wiring was the standard residential electrical method from the 1880s through the 1940s. It consists of single-insulated copper conductors run through porcelain knobs (for support) and tubes (for protection where wires pass through framing). There is no ground wire, and the system was designed for a fraction of today's electrical load.

Los Angeles has thousands of homes — especially in neighborhoods like Highland Park, Echo Park, Angelino Heights, and West Adams — with active K&T wiring. The wiring itself is not automatically dangerous when intact, but it is obsolete: it cannot safely support modern loads (HVAC, EV chargers, large appliances), lacks grounding, and is often compromised by insulation contact, splices, or age.

Many California insurers refuse to write or renew homeowner policies on homes with active K&T, and code requires replacement when walls are opened during renovation. If your home was built before 1950 and has never been rewired, K&T is likely present.

04 · Permits and code

Permit and Code Considerations for K&T Replacement in LA

  • Permit required. A whole-home or partial rewiring requires an LADBS electrical permit and a city inspection against the California Electrical Code.
  • Permit fees included. LADBS permit and inspection fees ($200–$450) are included in our all-in quotes.
  • Code compliance. The new wiring must meet current CEC requirements: grounding, GFCI/AFCI protection, and proper box fill.
  • Insurance documentation. The permit and inspection record supports insurance reinstatement and resale.

Los Angeles uses LADBS (Department of Building and Safety) for electrical permitting, with online filing through PermitLA and ePlanLA. Never hire a contractor who suggests skipping the permit on a rewire.

05 · Insurance and renovation

Insurance, Renovation, and Selling a Home with K&T Wiring

  • Insurance. Many California carriers refuse to write or renew policies on homes with active K&T, or charge higher premiums. Replacing the wiring can restore standard coverage.
  • Renovation trigger. Code requires replacement when walls are opened during a remodel, so bundling the rewire with a kitchen or bath remodel saves money.
  • Escrow. Inspectors flag K&T on reports; buyers often demand replacement or a credit. Replacing before listing removes the objection.
  • Documentation. AMY Electric provides the permit record and passed inspection report for insurance and resale.

07 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does knob and tube wiring replacement cost in Los Angeles?
Whole-home knob and tube wiring replacement in Los Angeles typically runs $8,000 to $15,000 or more, depending on home size, accessibility, and whether the service panel also needs upgrading. Per-circuit replacement runs $1,500 to $3,500. AMY Electric provides firm written quotes after a site visit, and the LADBS permit is included.
Is knob and tube wiring dangerous?
Knob and tube wiring is not inherently dangerous when intact, but it is obsolete: it lacks a ground wire, cannot safely support modern loads (HVAC, EV chargers, large appliances), and is often damaged by insulation, splices, or age. Many insurers refuse to cover homes with active knob and tube, and code requires replacement when walls are opened during renovation.
Do I need a permit to replace knob and tube wiring?
Yes. A whole-home or partial rewiring requires an LADBS electrical permit and a city inspection against the California Electrical Code. AMY Electric files the permit, schedules the inspection, and attends it. Permit and inspection fees ($200–$450) are included in our all-in quotes.
Will my insurance cover knob and tube wiring?
Many California insurers refuse to write or renew policies on homes with active knob and tube wiring, or charge higher premiums. Replacing the wiring can restore standard coverage. AMY Electric provides the permit record and passed inspection report that insurers request. Check with your carrier for any available credit.
How long does knob and tube replacement take?
A whole-home replacement typically takes three to seven days depending on home size and accessibility. Partial or single-circuit work takes one to two days. LADBS inspection follows completion. Older homes with plaster walls or limited attic access take longer.
Can I keep some knob and tube and replace the rest?
Partial replacement is possible when the remaining knob and tube is in good condition and not disturbed, but most whole-home projects replace everything for safety and insurability. AMY Electric assesses the full system during the site visit and recommends the safest, most cost-effective scope.
