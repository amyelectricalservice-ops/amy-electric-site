When You Need One

When Do You Need a Dedicated Circuit?

CEC requires dedicated circuits for:

  • Kitchen appliances — refrigerator, microwave, dishwasher
  • EV chargers — all Level 2 chargers need 40A–60A circuit. Learn more about EV charger installation →
  • Central AC / Heat pump
  • Electric range/oven — 50A
  • Clothes dryer — 30A
Cost Drivers

What Drives the Price of a Dedicated Circuit

Four variables move a circuit quote more than anything else: distance from the panel, panel capacity, wall construction, and the protection the code requires. A circuit run on an open stud wall ten feet from the panel sits at the bottom of every range; the same circuit fished through plaster across the house sits at the top.

Distance and routing

Wire is cheap; the labor to route it is not. Each foot of finished-wall fishing, attic crawl, or conduit run adds time. Runs that can use an attic, crawlspace, or unfinished garage wall cost the least because the wire path stays accessible and nothing needs patching.

Panel capacity and breaker space

A circuit needs two things at the panel: a breaker slot and available load capacity. A 100-amp panel already serving an EV charger, central AC, and an electric range is often at its calculated limit. The honest options there are a subpanel ($1,000–$2,500 installed) or a full panel upgrade ($2,500–$6,000) — not a tandem breaker that leaves the service overloaded.

Wall and finish repair

Drywall patching, stucco repair, and paint matching are real line items. A same-room run through an accessible wall may need no patching at all. A run through a plaster living-room wall usually does, and that is where quotes diverge most.

GFCI and AFCI protection

Kitchen, bath, garage, laundry, and outdoor circuits require GFCI protection; most living-area circuits now require AFCI. Dual-function breakers cost more than standard breakers, and the requirement is not optional under the California Electrical Code.

Permits & Timeline

Permits, Inspection and Timeline in Los Angeles

Every new circuit in the City of Los Angeles needs an LADBS electrical permit and a rough-in inspection before the wall is closed. Outside city limits — Burbank, Glendale, Pasadena, Santa Monica, Beverly Hills — the local building department issues its own permit; the process has the same shape with different fees and portal.

  • Permit cost: typically $150–$400 depending on valuation and jurisdiction, usually folded into a quoted price.
  • Scheduling: most single-circuit permits issue online within a few business days; inspections usually follow the next business day.
  • Labor time: a straightforward circuit is a half-day to a full day. Attic runs, conduit, and patching push it to two days.
  • What the inspector checks: wire gauge and type, breaker size and type, grounding and bonding, box fill, GFCI/AFCI protection, staple spacing, and the load calculation behind the breaker.

Unpermitted circuit work is the most common electrical defect found in LA pre-sale inspections. It has to be disclosed, often corrected, and can void a homeowner policy after a fire claim.

LA Homes

Dedicated Circuits in Los Angeles: Older Homes & New Loads

Los Angeles housing stock creates a specific pattern of dedicated-circuit needs. Pre-1970 bungalows in Los Angeles and Burbank often run kitchen receptacles, laundry, and garage outlets on two or three shared circuits — all high-draw, all on the same breakers. Valley tract homes from the 1960s–1980s add a second pattern: a 100A service that was adequate for gas appliances but has no headroom for an EV charger, induction range, or heat pump.

Retrofit reality: what the wall hides

Fishing a new circuit through plaster-and-lath or through a stucco exterior with no attic access costs more than running it over open studs in a remodel. That is why the cheapest time to add dedicated circuits is during a kitchen or garage remodel, when walls are open and the dedicated circuits service page → scope can share permit and drywall repair with the larger project. We price service upgrades and subpanels as alternates on the same quote so you can see the trade-off.

Bundling to save

  • Run kitchen remodel circuits plus EV charger circuit in one pull — per-circuit labor drops 25–40% when the route is shared.
  • Pair a subpanel with any two or more circuits; the subpanel cost is fixed, so the marginal circuit cost falls sharply.
  • Schedule the LADBS rough-in inspection once for all circuits, not per circuit.

For homes served by SCE outside LADBS jurisdiction — for example Pasadena — permit fees and lead times differ but the dedicated-circuit rule is the same. Every utility territory in LA County enforces the California Electrical Code requirement for dedicated high-draw circuits.

Questions

Dedicated Circuit FAQ

How much does a dedicated circuit cost in Los Angeles? ▾
A 15-amp dedicated circuit costs $250–$500, while a 50-amp circuit for an EV charger or range costs $500–$1,000. If a subpanel is needed to add capacity, expect $1,000–$2,500. Costs vary based on amperage, wiring distance from the panel, and whether the wall needs to be opened for new wiring.
Which appliances require a dedicated circuit? ▾
The California Electrical Code requires dedicated circuits for refrigerators, microwaves, dishwashers, all Level 2 EV chargers (40A–60A), central AC/heat pumps, electric ranges (50A), and clothes dryers (30A). Running high-draw appliances on shared circuits causes nuisance tripping and can overheat wiring.
Can I install a dedicated circuit without a permit in LA? ▾
No. New circuit installation in Los Angeles requires an LADBS permit and inspection. Working without a permit can result in fines, failed home sales, and voided insurance. A licensed C-10 electrician handles all permitting as part of the project.
How long does it take to install a dedicated circuit? ▾
Most single circuits are a half-day to a full day of labor. Add time for an attic or crawlspace route, surface conduit, or drywall patching. The permit itself usually issues within a few business days, and inspection typically follows the next business day after the work is complete.
Can two appliances share one dedicated circuit? ▾
Only if the circuit is sized for the combined load and the code allows it, which is rare for the appliances that actually require dedicated circuits. A 20-amp circuit cannot carry a microwave and a space heater together, and sharing defeats the purpose: one tripped breaker should never take down two appliances. Keep each high-draw appliance on its own circuit.
Do I need a dedicated circuit for a home office or space heater? ▾
A typical home office with a laptop, monitor, and printer runs fine on existing general-purpose circuits. A 1,500-watt space heater draws about 12.5 amps continuously and should have its own 20-amp circuit, especially in older homes where bedroom circuits are shared. Continuous loads at more than 80% of a breaker's rating are exactly what the code is written to prevent.
