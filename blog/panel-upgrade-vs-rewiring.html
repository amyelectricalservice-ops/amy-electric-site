Panel Upgrade vs Whole-Home Rewiring: Which Do You Need?

These two jobs are often confused. A panel upgrade replaces the breaker box; rewiring replaces the wires inside your walls. You may need one, both, or neither. Here's how to tell.

🔨

Panel Upgrade

Replaces the breaker panel (often to 200 amps). Fixes capacity, frequent trips, and outdated panels (FPE/Zinsco). Walls stay closed.

🔧

Whole-Home Rewire

Replaces the branch wiring throughout the house. Required for knob-and-tube, aluminum, or damaged insulation. Involves opening walls.

⚖

Both

Common in 1950s-70s LA homes: a new panel plus partial rewiring of the highest-risk circuits.

Decision Guide

Which One Do You Need?

1
Breakers trip under normal load

Likely a panel capacity or defect issue - start with a panel upgrade.

2
Two-prong or cloth-covered wires

Signals old wiring - a rewire (or at least partial) is the safe fix.

3
Adding EVs, AC, or a hot tub

Usually needs a panel upgrade for capacity, not a full rewire.

Questions

Panel vs Rewire FAQ

Is a panel upgrade cheaper than rewiring? ▾
Significantly. A panel upgrade runs $2,500-$4,500; a full rewire runs $10,000-$25,000 depending on home size.
Can I just upgrade the panel and skip rewiring? ▾
If your wiring is safe and up to code, yes. But if knob-and-tube or aluminum wiring is present, the inspector will require remediation before power is restored.
Will rewiring require opening all my walls? ▾
Often not all of them. We use fishing techniques and strategic access points to minimize drywall cuts; see our no-walls guide.
Do I need a permit for both? ▾
Yes. Both require a permit and final inspection by the city or LADWP.
How do I know which my home needs? ▾
Book a safety inspection. We evaluate the panel, wiring type, and load to recommend the minimum safe scope.
