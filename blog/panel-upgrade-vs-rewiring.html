Panel Upgrade vs Whole-Home
Rewiring: Which Do You Need?

By Amy, Licensed C-10 Electrician · over a decade serving Los Angeles

Homeowners often use these terms interchangeably, but they solve very different problems. This guide helps you understand what each involves and which one your home needs.

Published: 2026-04-15 · Updated: 2026-06-20

Comparison

What Each Job Actually Replaces

These two jobs are often confused. A panel upgrade replaces the breaker box; rewiring replaces the wires inside your walls. You may need one, both, or neither. Here's how to tell.

🔨

Panel Upgrade

Replaces the breaker panel (often to 200 amps). Fixes capacity, frequent trips, and outdated panels (FPE/Zinsco). Walls stay closed.

🔧

Whole-Home Rewire

Replaces the branch wiring throughout the house. Required for knob-and-tube, aluminum, or damaged insulation. Involves opening walls.

⚖

Both

Common in 1950s-70s LA homes: a new panel plus partial rewiring of the highest-risk circuits.

Decision Guide

Which One Do You Need?

1
Breakers trip under normal load

Likely a panel capacity or defect issue - start with a panel upgrade.

2
Two-prong or cloth-covered wires

Signals old wiring - a rewire (or at least partial) is the safe fix.

3
Adding EVs, AC, or a hot tub

Usually needs a panel upgrade for capacity, not a full rewire.

Telling Them Apart

Signs You Need a Rewire, Not Just a Panel

A panel upgrade fixes capacity and modernizes overcurrent protection. It cannot fix conductors that are themselves unsafe. These findings point at the wiring, not the box:

  • Knob-and-tube wiring anywhere in the living space — brittle insulation, no equipment ground, and a known fire risk when buried in insulation.
  • Aluminum branch-circuit wiring (common in LA homes built roughly 1965–1978). The failure mode is at the connections, not the panel, so a new panel alone does not resolve it.
  • Cloth or rubber insulation that crumbles when touched, or brittle conductors at every device box.
  • Two-prong receptacles throughout with no ground path — a symptom of ungrounded branch circuits, not a receptacle problem.
  • Scorched or melted insulation at multiple outlets, or a burning smell that returns after a breaker reset.
  • Decades of tapped additions — three or four splices inside a single box, no junction covers, circuits extended room by room.

Conversely, these usually mean the wiring is serviceable and the panel is the bottleneck: a 100-amp service that trips when the AC and an EV charger run together, a Zinsco or Federal Pacific panel, or no breaker space left for a new circuit.

Sequencing

Can You Do the Panel Now and Rewire Later?

Yes, and for many 1950s–1970s LA homes that is the right sequence. A new 200-amp panel with modern AFCI and GFCI breakers immediately improves safety on every circuit it feeds, and it creates the capacity that a later rewire needs anyway. Rewiring a house behind a 100-amp panel means doing the service work twice.

The caveat: if the inspection finds knob-and-tube or aluminum branch wiring, most jurisdictions will not sign off on a panel replacement until the unsafe circuits are remediated. In that case the honest scope is panel plus partial rewire in one permitted project. Ask for the inspection findings in writing before you approve either scope.

Permits and inspections

Both jobs need a permit and a final inspection — LADBS inside the City of Los Angeles, the local building department in Burbank, Glendale, Pasadena, Santa Monica, and Beverly Hills. Panel replacements also require a temporary disconnect and coordination with LADWP or the municipal utility for the service reconnect, which is why a panel swap is a scheduled day rather than an afternoon.

Living through the work

A panel upgrade is one to two days with a planned outage of a few hours. A whole-home rewire is measured in weeks: crews work room by room, power stays on for most of the project, and drywall patching plus paint follow the electrical rough-in. Budget for the patching and paint as separate line items — they are not electrical work, and quotes that omit them are not comparable.

Questions

Panel vs Rewire FAQ

Is a panel upgrade cheaper than rewiring? ▾
Significantly. A panel upgrade runs $2,500-$4,500; a full rewire runs $10,000-$25,000 depending on home size.
Can I just upgrade the panel and skip rewiring? ▾
If your wiring is safe and up to code, yes. But if knob-and-tube or aluminum wiring is present, the inspector will require remediation before power is restored.
Will rewiring require opening all my walls? ▾
Often not all of them. We use fishing techniques and strategic access points to minimize drywall cuts; see our no-walls guide.
Do I need a permit for both? ▾
Yes. Both require a permit and final inspection by the city or LADWP.
How do I know which my home needs? ▾
Book a safety inspection. We evaluate the panel, wiring type, and load to recommend the minimum safe scope.
How long does a panel upgrade take compared with a rewire? ▾
A panel upgrade is one to two days on site, with a planned outage of a few hours for the service disconnect. A whole-home rewire runs one to four weeks depending on size and wall construction, and drywall patching plus paint add another few days after the electrical rough-in passes inspection.
Does a panel upgrade fix aluminum wiring? ▾
No. Aluminum branch-circuit wiring fails at the device connections, not at the panel. A new panel improves capacity and overcurrent protection but leaves the aluminum conductors and their connections untouched. The fix is remediation at every device, or replacing the affected circuits outright.
Does a whole-home rewire include a new panel? ▾
Not automatically, and the quote should say so explicitly. Most full rewires do include a panel replacement because the old panel cannot carry the new circuits and AFCI protection, but it is a separate line item. Ask for the panel scope in writing so you can compare bids.
