How to Tell If Your Panel Needs Upgrading

Your electrical panel is the heart of your home. If it is old, overloaded, or showing signs of stress, it can become a safety risk and a major limitation for modern appliances and EV charging.

Why this matters

Many Los Angeles homes still run on older 60A or 100A service. That may have been fine when the house was built, but modern life demands far more. Today’s homes run central AC, EV chargers, home offices, kitchen appliances, and multiple electronics all at once. If your panel is outdated, it can trip breakers, overheat, or fail before it should.

Knowing the signs early helps you avoid damage to your home, nuisance outages, and safety issues. A panel upgrade is one of the most practical investments you can make for long-term reliability.

Top signs your electrical panel needs an upgrade

1. Breakers trip often

If your breaker trips whenever the AC, microwave, dryer, or EV charger turns on, your panel may be overloaded or losing capacity. Frequent tripping is one of the clearest warning signs.

2. Lights flicker or dim

Lights that dim when large appliances start are a sign the panel is struggling with voltage drops. This often shows up in older houses with undersized service.

3. The panel feels hot or smells unusual

If the panel is warm to the touch, shows discoloration, or gives off a burning odor, stop using it and schedule an inspection immediately. Those symptoms can signal a serious fire risk.

4. There are scorch marks or damaged breakers

Burn marks around breaker handles or arcing signs are not normal. They suggest the panel is aging or has loose connections and should be inspected by a licensed electrician.

5. You have an old panel

Panels more than 25-40 years old should be evaluated. Some older brands are especially risky and may need replacement even if they still work.

6. You are adding major electrical loads

If you are installing a new EV charger, adding an ADU, upgrading the HVAC, or remodeling the kitchen, your electrical demand may exceed what your current panel can safely support.

7. You rely on extension cords and power strips

If your home has too few dedicated outlets and you depend on plug strips to handle everyday demands, the electrical system may be stretched beyond its intended capacity.

8. You have outdated or unsupported brands

Older electrical systems, some fuse panels, or legacy breaker brands may not meet today’s safety standards. Replacement is often the safer and smarter option.

What a panel upgrade can solve

A proper panel upgrade can reduce nuisance breaker trips, support EV charger installation, improve reliability, and increase a home’s safety. It also avoids the pain of overloading circuits during peak usage.

In Los Angeles, many homes are seeing greater electrical loads because of central cooling, kitchen upgrades, home offices, and EV adoption. A modern 200A service is often a practical standard for medium to large single-family homes.

When to call a licensed electrician

Schedule an inspection if your breaker box is warm, if breakers are failing, if you are adding high-demand equipment, or if you still have an older panel that may no longer be code-compliant. The goal is to prevent safety problems rather than react after a failure.

FAQ

Common panel upgrade questions

What are the biggest warning signs that my panel needs upgrading? ▾
Frequent breaker trips, flickering lights, warm panel boxes, scorch marks, rising electrical demand, and a panel with 25+ years of service are all common signs.
How do I know if my breaker panel is overloaded? ▾
If your breakers trip when multiple appliances run or if your panel is warm, it is often overloaded. Our electricians can measure the load and recommend the right service size.
Should I upgrade my panel before installing an EV charger? ▾
Often yes. Level 2 EV chargers need dedicated capacity, and an upgrade may be necessary if your panel is already near its limit.
Can a panel upgrade improve safety? ▾
Yes. Replacing an outdated panel can reduce fire risk, improve reliability, and support modern electrical needs like AC, EV chargers, and home offices.
