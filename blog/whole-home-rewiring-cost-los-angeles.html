Wiring Types

Types of Wiring That Need Replacement

🔴

Knob-and-Tube Wiring

Found in homes built before 1940. Uses individual hot and neutral conductors through ceramic knobs. No ground wire. Fire hazard. Many insurers won't cover homes with active K&T wiring.

🟠

Aluminum Wiring

Used in homes built 1965–1973. Prone to loose connections at outlets and switches, causing overheating and fire risk. Requires special COPALUM or AlumiConn connectors at every connection point.

🟡

Ungrounded Romex (2-Wire)

Found in homes built 1940–1960. Has insulated conductors but no equipment ground. Limits outlet types and creates safety hazards for modern electronics and appliances.

🟢

Undersized Wiring

Older homes with 14-gauge or undersized wiring on 20A circuits. Can't handle modern electrical loads, causing overheating and fire risk.

Process

What to Expect During Whole-Home Rewiring

1
Inspection & Assessment

We inspect your entire electrical system, document existing wiring types, and develop a rewiring plan with you.

2
Permit Application

Whole-home rewiring requires an LADBS permit. We handle all paperwork and schedule the inspection timeline.

3
Wall Access

We access walls through attic, crawl space, or small openings in drywall. Minimal patching required when done by experienced electricians.

4
New Wiring Installation

All circuits are run with modern 12-gauge or 10-gauge Romex with proper grounding. New outlets, switches, and junction boxes installed.

5
Panel Upgrade

Old fuse boxes or undersized panels are replaced with modern 200A breaker panels during the rewiring process.

6
Testing & Inspection

Every circuit is tested for proper grounding, GFCI/AFCI protection, and load capacity. City inspector verifies code compliance.

Signs You Need Rewiring

Signs Your Home Needs Rewiring

🔥

Burning Smell

A burning or hot plastic smell from outlets, switches, or your panel is an immediate safety concern. Stop using the circuit and call an electrician.

💡

Flickering Lights

Lights that flicker or dim when appliances cycle on indicate inadequate wiring capacity or loose connections.

🔌

Two-Prong Outlets

Ungrounded two-prong outlets throughout the home signal outdated wiring that can't support modern electrical needs.

🧊

Frequent Breaker Trips

If breakers trip regularly under normal household loads, your wiring may be undersized or deteriorated.

📋

Insurance Requirements

Many homeowner insurance companies require rewiring of homes with knob-and-tube or aluminum wiring as a condition of coverage.

🏠

Home Purchase

If you're buying a home with outdated wiring, rewiring before moving in avoids costly surprises and ensures code compliance.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About Whole-Home Rewiring Costs

How much does whole-home rewiring cost in Los Angeles? ▾
Whole-home rewiring in Los Angeles typically costs $8,000 to $30,000 depending on home size, wiring condition, and accessibility. A 1,000 sq ft home runs $8,000–$15,000, a 1,500 sq ft home costs $12,000–$22,000, and homes over 2,000 sq ft can range from $18,000 to $30,000 or more. Knob-and-tube and aluminum wiring replacements are at the higher end.
How long does whole-home rewiring take? ▾
Most whole-home rewiring projects take 3 to 10 days depending on home size, wall accessibility, and whether drywall needs to be opened. Larger homes or those with limited attic/crawl space access may take up to two weeks. You may need to vacate the home for part of the project.
Do I need to move out during rewiring? ▾
For most whole-home rewiring projects, you will need to vacate for at least 2 to 5 days during the most invasive phase when walls are open and circuits are being run. Some homeowners stay elsewhere for the full project. Your electrician can advise on a specific timeline for your home.
What are the signs my home needs rewiring? ▾
Common signs include: frequent breaker trips, flickering or dimming lights, burning smell from outlets or panels, two-prong ungrounded outlets throughout the home, discolored or warm outlet covers, aluminum or knob-and-tube wiring, and insurance companies requiring rewiring as a condition of coverage.
Does rewiring increase home value? ▾
Yes. Whole-home rewiring significantly increases property value and buyer appeal. A modern electrical system eliminates a major inspection objection, may reduce homeowner insurance premiums, and makes the home eligible for modern electrical panel upgrades, EV charger installation, and smart home systems.
