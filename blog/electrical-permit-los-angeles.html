Permit Guide

Do You Need an Electrical Permit in Los Angeles?

In Los Angeles, most electrical work requires a permit from LADBS or your local city building department.

When Permits Are Needed

When Do You Need an Electrical Permit?

  • New circuits or wiring
    Adding any new electrical circuit requires a permit — including EV chargers, new outlets, or subpanels.
  • Panel upgrades or replacements
    Any work on your main electrical panel requires a permit and inspection.
  • EV charger installation
    All Level 2 EV charger installations require an electrical permit. Permitted work qualifies for LADWP rebate.
  • Whole-home rewiring
    Rewiring requires a permit with multiple inspections at rough-in and final stages.
  • Generator transfer switch
    Installing a transfer switch requires a permit and utility coordination.
  • Structural electrical modifications
    Moving the meter, relocating the panel, or adding service capacity requires permits.

Why Permits Matter

Why Permitted Work Matters

  • Insurance risk — Unpermitted work may not be covered by homeowners insurance if it causes a fire
  • Home sale issues — Unpermitted work is frequently flagged during inspections, creating liability
  • Safety — Electrical inspections verify code compliance. Without them, faulty work goes undetected
  • Rebate eligibility — LADWP rebates require permitted installations with proof of inspection

Questions

Electrical Permit FAQ

Do I need a permit for electrical work in Los Angeles? ▾
Yes. Most electrical work in LA requires an LADBS permit, including new circuits, panel upgrades, EV charger installations, rewiring, and generator transfer switches. Simple like-for-like replacements (swapping an outlet or switch) typically don't require a permit. Always check with your electrician about permit requirements.
How much does an electrical permit cost in LA? ▾
LADBS residential electrical permits range from $150–$500 depending on the scope. Minor work like adding a circuit costs $150–$300, while panel upgrades run $200–$500. Complex projects requiring plan check can cost $300–$1,000+. AMY Electric includes all permit fees in our project quotes.
Does unpermitted electrical work affect home sales in LA? ▾
Yes. Unpermitted electrical work is frequently discovered during buyer inspections and can delay or derail sales. Buyers may demand the work be permitted retroactively (which requires inspection and possible rework), negotiate a lower price, or walk away. Permitted work protects your home's value and provides documentation of code compliance.
