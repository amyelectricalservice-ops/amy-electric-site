Cost drivers

What Drives the Price Up or Down

Two homes on the same block can pay different totals for the same 200-amp upgrade. These are the factors that move the number, each with its published range from the table above:

The amperage jump you're making

100A to 200A runs $2,500–$4,500 with a standard 30–42 space panel. Going 200A to 400A for a large home runs $4,500–$8,000 for dual-panel or 320A continuous service.

A hazardous brand under the cover

Zinsco and Federal Pacific replacements run $2,800–$4,800 because replacing the panel is only half the job — the remediation logic is documented on our Zinsco and FPE panel hazards page.

Meter base and weatherhead condition

If the meter base or weatherhead on the outside of the house also needs replacement, that adds labor and utility coordination to the visit — the 200-amp service page lists it as a main cost variable along with panel location.

Grounding reality in older homes

Homes with knob-and-tube wiring or ungrounded branch circuits may require grounding remediation before the new panel can pass LADBS inspection, which the upgrade quote will spell out as a line item if it applies.

Permit and utility fee line items

LADBS permit and inspection fees run $200–$450 and are included in AMY Electric's all-inclusive quotes. The LADWP/SCE service connection fee ($300–$600) is billed separately for the line upgrade or meter spot rework.

The subpanel alternative

If the main panel is healthy but you need circuits for an ADU, garage, or addition, a new 60A–100A subpanel runs $800–$2,000. The subpanel installation service covers the option without a full main-panel swap.

Offset on the other side of the ledger: qualifying electric panel upgrades earn the federal 25C energy-efficient home improvement credit at 30%, up to $600, shown as the green row above.

Signs You Need an Upgrade

Signs It's Time to Upgrade Your Panel

🧊

Frequently Tripping Breakers

If breakers trip regularly when multiple appliances run simultaneously, your panel likely lacks capacity.

💡

Flickering Lights

Lights dimming when AC, dryer, or oven turns on indicates your panel is near its load limit.

🔌

Two-Prong Outlets

Two-prong (ungrounded) outlets throughout the home often signal outdated wiring needing replacement.

📅

Panel Over 40 Years Old

Federal Pacific, Zinsco, and other older panels manufactured before 1990 are known safety hazards.

🚗

Planning an EV Charger

Most EV chargers require a 50A dedicated circuit. If your panel lacks space or capacity, an upgrade is needed.

🏠

Adding Major Appliances

New AC units, heat pumps, tankless water heaters, or kitchen renovations often require a panel upgrade.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About Panel Upgrade Costs

How much does a 200 amp panel upgrade cost in Los Angeles? ▾
Most Los Angeles homeowners spend between $2,500 and $4,500 for a 100A to 200A panel upgrade, which includes the permit, labor, materials, and inspection. The exact cost depends on your home's wiring condition, the complexity of the panel swap, and whether additional work like a meter upgrade or LADWP coordination is needed.
Do I need a permit for a panel upgrade in Los Angeles? ▾
Yes. All electrical panel upgrades in Los Angeles require a permit from LADBS (Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety). A city inspector must verify the work meets the California Electrical Code before the project is considered complete. AMY Electric handles all permit paperwork and inspection coordination for you.
How long does a panel upgrade take from start to finish? ▾
The physical panel replacement itself takes 4 to 8 hours for a standard upgrade. However, the full process including permit application, LADWP coordination for the power shutoff, installation, and final city inspection typically takes 1 to 3 weeks from start to finish.
Does a panel upgrade qualify for the federal 25C tax credit? ▾
Yes — qualifying electric panel upgrades are covered by the federal 25C energy-efficient home improvement credit at 30%, up to $600, which the breakdown table above lists as a line item. Keep the itemized quote plus your permit and inspection records so your tax preparer can document the qualifying equipment.
Should I upgrade my panel before installing an EV charger? ▾
Likely, if your panel is 100 amps. Level 2 EV chargers draw 30 to 50 amps, and a 100-amp panel was sized for a home with lights plus a few appliances — add a charger, heat pump HVAC, or major appliances and you generally need the 200-amp upgrade. We confirm with a load calculation during the free estimate, and the charger's own dedicated circuit is part of the EV charger installation project.
How much does a 200 to 400 amp upgrade cost? ▾
It runs $4,500 to $8,000, for large homes needing dual-panel service or 320A continuous capacity, per the breakdown table above. It is the right scope when EV charging, larger HVAC, and main living circuits together exceed what 200 amps can carry — the load calculation in the quote establishes which scope applies.
