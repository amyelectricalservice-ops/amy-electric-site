Upgrading your electrical panel is one of the most common electrical projects for Los Angeles homes. Whether you need more capacity for modern appliances, EV charging, or an aging panel replacement, here's what you'll pay in 2026.

Signs You Need an Upgrade

Signs It's Time to Upgrade Your Panel

🧊

Frequently Tripping Breakers

If breakers trip regularly when multiple appliances run simultaneously, your panel likely lacks capacity.

💡

Flickering Lights

Lights dimming when AC, dryer, or oven turns on indicates your panel is near its load limit.

🔌

Two-Prong Outlets

Two-prong (ungrounded) outlets throughout the home often signal outdated wiring needing replacement.

📅

Panel Over 40 Years Old

Federal Pacific, Zinsco, and other older panels manufactured before 1990 are known safety hazards.

🚗

Planning an EV Charger

Most EV chargers require a 50A dedicated circuit. If your panel lacks space or capacity, an upgrade is needed.

🏠

Adding Major Appliances

New AC units, heat pumps, tankless water heaters, or kitchen renovations often require a panel upgrade.

Upgrade Process

What to Expect During a Panel Upgrade

1
Load Calculation

We assess your current and future electrical needs to determine the right panel size.

2
Permit with LADBS

All panel upgrades in LA require a permit. We handle the application and scheduling.

3
Power Shutdown

We coordinate with LADWP or your local utility for a brief planned outage during the swap.

4
Panel Replacement

We remove the old panel, install the new one, route all circuits, and label everything clearly.

5
City Inspection

City inspector verifies code compliance. We attend and address any issues immediately.

Is It Worth It?

Is a Panel Upgrade Worth the Investment?

A 200-amp panel upgrade typically costs $2,500–$4,500 and offers high ROI. Beyond eliminating tripped breakers and dimming lights, a modern 200-amp panel:

  • Increases property value and buyer appeal
  • Enables EV charger installation
  • Supports modern appliances and smart home systems
  • Reduces fire risk from outdated or overloaded panels
  • May lower homeowner insurance premiums
