Signs Your Los Angeles Home
Needs Rewiring

By Amy, Licensed C-10 Electrician · 15+ years serving Los Angeles

Older Los Angeles homes often have outdated wiring that poses serious fire risks and can't handle modern electrical demands. Learn the warning signs that indicate it's time for a professional rewiring assessment.

Why Rewiring Matters

Why Los Angeles Homes Need Rewiring

Los Angeles has one of the oldest housing stocks in California. Many homes in neighborhoods like Silver Lake, Echo Park, Highland Park, and the Hollywood Hills were built in the 1920s-1960s with wiring that was adequate for that era but is now outdated and potentially dangerous. The National Fire Protection Association reports that electrical failures are a leading cause of house fires, and outdated wiring is a primary culprit.

🔥

Fire Safety

Outdated wiring is a leading cause of residential fires. Aluminum wiring, knob-and-tube, and cloth-covered wiring all present elevated fire risks that increase with age and wear.

⚡

Modern Electrical Loads

Today's homes use 3-4x more electricity than homes built 50 years ago. EV chargers, heat pumps, induction ranges, and home offices all demand capacity that old wiring wasn't designed to provide.

🏠

Insurance Compliance

Many insurance companies now require documentation that aluminum or knob-and-tube wiring has been replaced before they'll issue or renew a homeowner's policy. Some won't cover homes with these wiring types at all.

💰

Home Value

A fully rewired home commands a higher sale price and is easier to sell. Buyers and inspectors look for modern wiring as a sign that the home has been properly maintained and is safe.

Warning Signs

Top 10 Signs Your Home Needs Rewiring

Watch for these critical indicators that your home's wiring may need professional attention. If you notice any of these signs, schedule an inspection with a licensed electrician.

⚠️

1. Aluminum Wiring (1965-1973)

Homes built between 1965 and 1973 often have aluminum branch circuit wiring, which is a known fire hazard. Aluminum expands and contracts with temperature changes, causing loose connections that can overheat and arc. If your home was built during this period, have it inspected immediately.

⚠️

2. Knob-and-Tube Wiring

Common in homes built before 1950, knob-and-tube wiring uses ceramic insulators and lacks a ground wire. It's not inherently dangerous when in good condition, but insulation, age, and modifications can create serious hazards. Most insurers require replacement.

💡

3. Flickering or Dimming Lights

Lights that flicker, dim, or brighten unexpectedly—especially when appliances cycle on and off—indicate loose connections, undersized wiring, or deteriorating insulation. This is a common sign of wiring that needs attention.

🔥

4. Burning Smells or Discoloration

A burning plastic smell, scorch marks around outlets or switches, or discolored faceplates are serious warning signs. These indicate overheating wiring that could start a fire. If you notice these signs, turn off the circuit and call an electrician immediately.

🖐️

5. Warm Outlets or Switch Plates

Outlets or switch plates that feel warm to the touch—or that are too hot to touch—indicate loose connections or overloaded circuits. This is a fire hazard that requires immediate professional attention.

🔌

6. Two-Prong Outlets

Two-prong outlets (no ground wire) are common in older homes. While they work for basic devices, they provide no protection against electrical shock and are incompatible with modern three-prong plugs. A grounded wiring system is essential for safety.

⚡

7. Frequent Breaker Trips

If your breakers trip regularly—even under normal use—it may indicate that your wiring is deteriorating, circuits are overloaded, or connections are loose. While occasional trips are normal, frequent tripping is a warning sign.

📉

8. Insurance Issues

If your insurance company is asking about your wiring, refusing to renew your policy, or requiring a wiring inspection, take it seriously. They've identified your home as a higher risk, and they're often right.

🔧

9. Cloth-Covered Wiring

Many homes built before 1960 have cloth-covered (Raybestos) wiring. The rubber insulation inside the cloth covering degrades over time, becoming brittle and cracking. This exposes bare copper and creates a fire and shock hazard.

📅

10. Home Over 50 Years Old (No Updates)

If your home is over 50 years old and the wiring has never been updated, it's time for a professional assessment. Even if you haven't noticed symptoms, the wiring may be deteriorating in ways that aren't immediately visible.

What To Expect

The Rewiring Process: What to Expect

Whole-home rewiring is a significant project, but understanding the process helps you plan and reduces stress. Here's what a typical rewiring project involves.

1
Professional Assessment

A licensed electrician inspects your entire electrical system, identifies all wiring that needs replacement, and provides a detailed scope of work and quote. This includes checking your panel, circuits, outlets, switches, and junction boxes.

2
Permit Pulling

Whole-home rewiring requires an electrical permit from your local building department. Your electrician handles all permitting and coordinates inspections with the city or county.

3
Wall Access

To run new wiring through finished walls, small access holes are cut in drywall or plaster. These are typically 4-6 inches in diameter and are repaired and patched after wiring is complete.

4
New Wiring Installation

Modern NM-B (Romex) cable is run from the panel to every outlet, switch, and fixture location. A new ground wire is included throughout, providing the safety grounding that older wiring lacks.

5
Panel Upgrade

Most rewiring projects include a panel upgrade to 200A service, as the new wiring and modern electrical loads require more capacity than older panels can provide.

6
Inspection & Finish

The city inspects the rough-in wiring before walls are closed. After inspection, walls are patched, painted, and new outlets, switches, and fixtures are installed. Final inspection verifies everything is code-compliant.

Cost & Timeline

Rewiring Cost and Timeline in Los Angeles

Rewiring costs vary based on home size, accessibility, and the type of wiring being replaced. Here's what Los Angeles homeowners typically expect.

🏠

Small Home (Under 1,500 sq ft)

$8,000–$12,000
Typical for bungalows, small ranch homes, and cottages. Usually 2-4 weeks to complete. May be less disruptive if there's attic or crawl space access.

🏡

Medium Home (1,500–2,500 sq ft)

$12,000–$16,000
Typical for most single-family homes. Usually 3-5 weeks to complete. Access to walls through attic or crawl space reduces wall damage.

🏘️

Large Home (2,500+ sq ft)

$16,000–$20,000+
Typical for large homes, multi-story homes, or homes with complex layouts. Usually 4-6 weeks to complete. May include sub-panel installation.

Note: These are rough estimates. Actual costs depend on your home's specific conditions, accessibility, and the scope of work. A professional assessment is needed for an accurate quote.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my house needs rewiring? ▾
Key signs include: aluminum or knob-and-tube wiring, frequent breaker trips, flickering lights, burning smells, warm outlets or switch plates, discolored outlets, two-prong outlets (no ground), and insurance companies refusing coverage. If your home was built before 1970, a professional inspection is strongly recommended.
How much does whole-home rewiring cost in Los Angeles? ▾
Whole-home rewiring in Los Angeles typically costs $8,000-$20,000 depending on home size, accessibility of wiring paths, and the type of wiring being replaced. Homes with knob-and-tube or aluminum wiring may be on the higher end. A professional assessment is needed for an accurate quote.
Is rewiring a house worth it? ▾
Yes, for homes with outdated or hazardous wiring. Rewiring eliminates fire risks, allows you to add modern electrical loads (EV chargers, appliances), increases home value, and may be required by your insurance company. Many LA homes built before 1970 still have hazardous wiring that needs replacement.
