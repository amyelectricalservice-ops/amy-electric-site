01 · What an FPE panel is

What a Federal Pacific (FPE) Panel Is and Why It Must Be Replaced

Federal Pacific Electric (FPE) Stab-Lok panels were installed in hundreds of thousands of homes across the United States from the 1950s through the 1980s, including a large share of Los Angeles housing stock built in that era. The Stab-Lok breaker design has a documented history of failing to trip during overcurrent conditions, which can allow branch-circuit wiring to overheat and ignite.

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) investigated FPE breakers, and independent testing has shown failure-to-trip rates far above acceptable limits. No breaker swap, no "fix," and no maintenance procedure makes an FPE panel safe. Replacement is the only compliant path.

Insurance carriers in California increasingly refuse to write or renew homeowner policies on homes with FPE panels, and home inspectors flag them on nearly every report. If you see "Stab-Lok" on the breaker handle or "Federal Pacific" on the panel label, the panel is in the hazardous class and should be replaced.

04 · Permits and code

Permit and Code Considerations for FPE Replacement in LA

  • Permit required. A panel replacement requires an LADBS electrical permit and a city inspection against the California Electrical Code. AMY Electric files the permit, schedules the inspection, and attends it.
  • Permit fees included. LADBS permit and inspection fees ($200–$450) are included in our all-in quotes.
  • Code compliance. The new panel must meet current CEC requirements, including grounding, bonding, and arc-fault protection where applicable.
  • Utility coordination. LADWP or SCE may need to upgrade the service connection for higher amperage; this runs parallel to our work.

Los Angeles uses LADBS (Department of Building and Safety) for electrical permitting, with online filing through PermitLA and ePlanLA. Never hire a contractor who suggests skipping the permit on a panel replacement.

05 · Insurance and escrow

Insurance, Escrow, and Selling a Home with an FPE Panel

FPE panels create real-world friction beyond safety:

  • Insurance. Many California carriers refuse to write or renew homeowner policies on homes with FPE panels, or charge higher premiums. Replacing the panel can restore standard rates and coverage.
  • Escrow. Home inspectors flag FPE panels on nearly every report, and buyers commonly demand replacement or a credit before closing. Replacing before listing removes the objection.
  • Documentation. AMY Electric provides the LADBS permit record and passed inspection report that insurers, buyers, and escrow officers request.
  • Tax credit. Qualifying panel upgrades may be eligible for the Federal 25C tax credit (up to $600, 30%). Consult your tax professional.
