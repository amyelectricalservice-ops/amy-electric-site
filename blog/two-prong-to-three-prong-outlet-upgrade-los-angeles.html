Cost Factors

What Drives the Cost of a Two-Prong to Three-Prong Upgrade in Los Angeles

  • Grounding method. True grounding requires a new ground wire; GFCI protection is cheaper but not a true ground.
  • Number of outlets. More outlets mean more labor but lower per-outlet cost.
  • Wiring access. Fishing new cable through finished walls adds labor.
  • Panel condition. An aging panel may need replacement before grounding upgrades.
  • Permit and inspection. LADBS fees are included in all-in quotes.

A site visit confirms the exact scope before any work is quoted.

02 · GFCI alternative

GFCI Protection as an Alternative to Grounding

  • Code-accepted. NEC allows GFCI protection on ungrounded circuits as an alternative when full rewiring is not feasible.
  • Labeled outlets. GFCI-protected ungrounded outlets must be labeled "No Equipment Ground."
  • Shock protection. GFCI provides shock protection but does not create a true ground.
  • Surge protectors. Surge protectors still need a true ground to function properly.
03 · Why two-prong

Why Older Homes Have Two-Prong Outlets

  • Pre-1960 homes. Ungrounded wiring was standard before the ground wire was required.
  • No ground wire. Two-prong outlets indicate ungrounded circuits.
  • Insurance. Many insurers require grounding upgrades or GFCI protection for coverage.
  • Modern appliances. Three-prong appliances expect a ground for safety.
04 · Permits and code

Permit and Code Considerations in LA

  • No permit for same-box swap. Replacing an outlet in the same box is typically permit-exempt.
  • Permit for new wiring. Running new cable or adding a circuit requires an LADBS electrical permit and inspection.
  • Permit fees included. When required, LADBS fees ($200–$450) are included in our all-in quotes.
  • Code compliance. Work must meet current California Electrical Code and NEC requirements.

Los Angeles uses LADBS for electrical permitting. Never hire a contractor who suggests skipping a permit on new wiring work.

06 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a two-prong to three-prong outlet upgrade cost in Los Angeles?
A two-prong to three-prong outlet upgrade in Los Angeles costs $150 to $350 per outlet using GFCI protection, or $1,500 to $5,000 for whole-home grounding. Costs depend on the number of outlets and whether rewiring is needed. AMY Electric provides firm written quotes after a site visit.
Can I just replace a two-prong outlet with a three-prong outlet?
No, not without a ground. Installing a three-prong outlet on an ungrounded circuit is a code violation and a safety hazard. The proper options are GFCI protection (labeled "No Equipment Ground") or a full grounding upgrade.
Is GFCI an acceptable alternative to grounding?
Yes. NEC allows GFCI protection on ungrounded circuits as an alternative, provided the outlet is labeled "No Equipment Ground." This provides shock protection but does not create a true ground for surge protectors.
Why do older homes have two-prong outlets?
Homes built before 1960 often have ungrounded wiring with two-prong outlets. The third prong (ground) was not required by code until later. These homes need grounding upgrades or GFCI protection for modern safety.
Do I need a permit for an outlet upgrade?
Replacing an outlet in the same box is usually permit-exempt. Running new wiring or adding a circuit requires an LADBS electrical permit and inspection. AMY Electric files any required permits and attends the inspection.
Is GFCI protection as good as grounding?
GFCI protection reduces shock risk but does not provide a true ground. Some appliances and surge protectors still need a ground to function. AMY Electric recommends based on the home and the insurer's requirements.
How long does an outlet upgrade take?
A single outlet takes 30 to 60 minutes; a whole-home upgrade takes 1 to 3 days. LADBS inspection follows if a permit is required.
