Cost Factors

What Drives the Cost of Whole-Home Surge Protection in Los Angeles

  • Surge protector type. Type 1 (service entrance) costs more than Type 2 (panel-level).
  • Panel condition. An aging panel may need replacement before installation.
  • Warranty. Longer-warranty devices cost more upfront.
  • Permits. Panel work requires an LADBS permit, included in all-in quotes.
  • Insurance. Some carriers discount premiums for whole-home surge protection.

A site visit confirms the exact scope before any work is quoted.

03 · Panel-level vs point-of-use

Panel-Level vs Point-of-Use Surge Protection

  • Panel-level (whole-home). Protects every circuit from a single device at the panel. Best first line of defense.
  • Point-of-use. Power strips and outlet protectors for individual devices. Supplementary, not a substitute.
  • Layered approach. Whole-home + point-of-use for high-value electronics (computers, AV equipment, smart panels).
  • Insurance. Some insurers offer discounts for documented whole-home surge protection.
04 · Insurance and warranty

Insurance, Warranty, and Surge Protection

  • Insurance discounts. Some California insurers offer premium discounts for whole-home surge protection.
  • Device warranty. Most whole-home surge protectors carry a connected-equipment warranty (often $25,000–$50,000).
  • Replacement. Surge protectors degrade over time. Replace after a major surge or every 5–10 years.
  • Documentation. AMY Electric provides the installation record insurers request.
05 · Permits and code

Permit and Code Considerations for Surge Protection in LA

  • No permit for panel install. Installing a surge protector in an existing panel is usually permit-exempt.
  • Permit for panel work. If panel upgrade or new circuit is required, an LADBS electrical permit and inspection are needed.
  • Permit fees included. When required, LADBS fees ($200–$450) are included in our all-in quotes.
  • Code compliance. Work must meet California Electrical Code requirements.

Los Angeles uses LADBS for electrical permitting. Never hire a contractor who suggests skipping a permit on panel work.

07 · FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does whole-home surge protection cost in Los Angeles?
Whole-home surge protection in Los Angeles costs $300 to $700 for installation of a Type 1 or Type 2 device. If panel work is required, costs run $800 to $1,500. Prices include the device, labor, and testing. AMY Electric provides firm written quotes after a site visit.
What is the difference between Type 1 and Type 2 surge protectors?
Type 1 protects against external surges (lightning, utility) and is installed at the service entrance. Type 2 protects against internal surges (appliances, HVAC) and is installed at the panel. Most homes benefit from Type 2; high-risk areas benefit from both.
Is whole-home surge protection worth it?
Yes. A single surge can destroy electronics, appliances, and smart-home devices worth thousands. A whole-home protector costs $300–$700 and protects every circuit. Many insurers offer discounts for documented surge protection.
Does surge protection help with insurance?
Some insurers offer premium discounts for whole-home surge protection, and many require it for high-value electronics coverage. AMY Electric provides the installation record insurers request.
Do I need a permit for surge protection installation?
Installing a surge protector in an existing panel is usually permit-exempt. If panel work or a new circuit is required, an LADBS electrical permit and inspection are needed. AMY Electric files any required permits.
How long does surge protection installation take?
30 to 60 minutes for a panel-level device; 2 to 4 hours if panel work or a new circuit is needed. LADBS inspection follows if a permit is required.
Is surge protection worth it for a home office?
Yes. A home office with computers, monitors, and networking gear is a high-value target for surges. Whole-home plus point-of-use protection is best practice.
