Before the install

What Every Install Needs Before the Charger Goes On

Whichever charger wins the comparison, the electrical work behind it is the same scope, and it is priced the same way:

  • A dedicated 240-volt circuit. Level 2 charging is a dedicated 240V circuit, sized for the charger's amperage — the 48A Wall Connector draws 30–50 amps at the top of the Level 2 range, more than most existing circuits spare.
  • A load calculation. The licensed electrician runs the load calculation and confirms whether your panel can accept the charger circuit or whether a 200-amp panel upgrade ($2,500–$4,500 for a 100A→200A jump) is the real starting point.
  • Permit and inspection. LADBS permits at $150–$400 are part of a compliant install, followed by inspection so the EVSE is legal and insurable.
  • LADWP rebate pre-approval. The process list for a compliant Level 2 install: load calculation, LADWP rebate pre-approval, permit, dedicated circuit, inspection.

Physically, a Level 2 installation takes 2 to 4 hours once the dedicated circuit exists. The EV charger installation service covers the full scope.

Cost range

Why the Total Cost Range Varies Between Homes

The installed table above spans real spread in Los Angeles housing stock. The variables:

  • Distance from panel to parking spot — a garage-adjacent panel is the cheap case; a run across the house adds wire, conduit and labor.
  • Panel capacity — if the load calculation finds no spare capacity, the EVSE circuit alone could overload the service; that is when the 100A→200A upgrade enters the quote.
  • Hardwired vs. plug-in. The Wall Connector is hardwired (its 48A output wants the higher-amperage circuit), and the published range for hardwired EVSE sits at $500–$1,200 — the same span as the Tesla total above once the charger hardware is included.
  • The plug-in alternative. A NEMA 14-50 outlet at $350–$600 lets any J1772 charger plug in, trading some charging speed for the cheapest install.

NACS Standard

Tesla NACS vs J1772: The Future

Starting 2024–2025, Ford, GM, Rivian, and others are adopting Tesla's NACS connector as standard. This means:

  • Tesla Wall Connector will be compatible with most future EVs natively
  • Current non-Tesla EVs need a J1772 charger or adapter
  • Our recommendation: Tesla owners → Wall Connector. Multi-brand → J1772 charger

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About EV Chargers

Which is better, Tesla Wall Connector or ChargePoint Home Flex? ▾
For Tesla owners, the Tesla Wall Connector is the best choice at $475 with 48A output, seamless Tesla ecosystem integration, and Wi-Fi connectivity for OTA updates. For households with multiple EV brands or non-Tesla vehicles, the ChargePoint Home Flex at $559 to $699 with J1772 connector is more versatile and works with all EVs on the market today.
Can I use a Tesla Wall Connector with a non-Tesla EV? ▾
The Tesla Wall Connector uses Tesla's NACS connector, which is not directly compatible with non-Tesla EVs that use the J1772 standard. You would need a J1772-to-NACS adapter (sold separately for about $150 to $250) or choose a J1772 charger instead. Starting in 2025, many non-Tesla EVs are adopting the NACS connector, which will make the Wall Connector natively compatible with those vehicles.
What is the best budget home EV charger? ▾
The Grizzl-E Classic at $399 is widely regarded as the best budget home EV charger. It delivers 40A of charging power, has a rugged NEMA 4 enclosure suitable for indoor or outdoor installation, and does not require Wi-Fi for basic operation. The Emporia EV Charger at $399 with 48A output and built-in energy monitoring is another excellent value option.
How much does it cost to install a Tesla Wall Connector in Los Angeles? ▾
$500 to $1,200 installed, charger plus the dedicated-circuit electrical work, per the table above. Add $150 to $400 in LADBS permit and inspection fees, and the price moves with the distance from your panel and whether the load calculation finds spare capacity.
Do I need a permit to install a home EV charger in Los Angeles? ▾
Yes. A compliant install is permit, inspection and dedicated circuit — the process list is load calculation, LADWP rebate pre-approval, LADBS permit, dedicated circuit, inspection. Skipping the permit is what turns a $150 fee line into an unpermitted-equipment problem at resale or claim time.
How long does the installation take? ▾
Level 2 installations take 2 to 4 hours once the dedicated circuit is in place. Homes that need the circuit built from scratch, or a panel upgrade before the charger can be fed, are scheduled accordingly — the estimate call tells you which case you are in.
