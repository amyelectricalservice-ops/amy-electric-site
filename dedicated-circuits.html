FAQ

Dedicated Circuit FAQ

Do I need a permit for a dedicated circuit in Los Angeles? ▾
Yes — installing a new dedicated circuit in Los Angeles requires a permit through the local building department, regardless of whether it is for a major appliance like a refrigerator, washing machine, or EV charger, or for a smaller dedicated circuit for a home office or entertainment system. Any new wiring that adds a circuit to your electrical panel requires a permit because it involves installing new cable or conduit from the panel to the outlet location, adding or replacing a breaker in the panel, and making connections that must be verified by a building inspector. The permit fee for a dedicated circuit typically ranges from $50 to $150 depending on your specific city within Los Angeles County. AMY Electric handles all permitting for every dedicated circuit installation we perform. The rough-in and final inspections that come with the permit provide important verification that the circuit is sized correctly for its intended load and that all connections are safe and code-compliant.
Can I install a dedicated circuit myself? ▾
While California law allows homeowners to perform electrical work on their own single-family dwelling through an owner-builder permit, there are significant practical and safety reasons to hire a licensed electrician for dedicated circuit installation. Installing a dedicated circuit requires pulling cable or conduit from the panel to the outlet location — often through attics, crawlspaces, or finished walls — making proper connections at both the breaker and the outlet, correctly sizing the wire gauge for the breaker amperage and the length of the run (voltage drop calculations), and ensuring the new circuit does not overload the panel's total capacity. Mistakes in any of these areas can create fire hazards that may not be immediately detectable. Additionally, dedicated circuit work requires permits and inspections, and if your work fails inspection, you are responsible for diagnosing and fixing the problem yourself. Most homeowners find that the relatively modest cost of professional installation is well worth the safety and peace of mind.
How do I know if my panel has capacity for a new circuit? ▾
Determining whether your panel has capacity for a new dedicated circuit requires two separate checks: physical space in the panel and electrical load capacity. First, check if your panel has an open breaker slot — if all slots are filled with breakers, you may need a tandem breaker (which fits two circuits in one slot) or a subpanel to add capacity. More importantly, your panel's total electrical load must be calculated per National Electrical Code Article 220 to ensure the new circuit will not overload the main service. This calculation involves adding up the existing loads (lighting, appliances, HVAC, etc.) plus the new circuit's expected load (typically 1,440 to 1,920 watts for a 15- or 20-amp dedicated circuit) and verifying the total does not exceed 80% of your main breaker rating. A licensed electrician can perform this load calculation quickly and determine whether a panel upgrade is needed before installing your new circuit.
What's the difference between a 240V outlet and a 120V outlet? ▾
The primary difference between 240V and 120V circuits is the voltage delivered and the applications they serve. A standard 120V outlet — the typical three-prong outlet found throughout most Los Angeles homes — uses one hot wire and one neutral wire and supplies up to 1,920 watts for 15-amp circuits or 2,400 watts for 20-amp circuits, making it suitable for lighting, electronics, small appliances, and general-purpose receptacles. A 240V outlet uses two hot wires (each carrying 120V out of phase) and delivers significantly more power — up to 4,800 watts for a 20-amp circuit, 7,200 watts for a 30-amp circuit, or 12,000 watts for a 50-amp circuit. 240V circuits are required for major appliances including EV chargers, electric ranges and ovens, central air conditioning units, electric water heaters, clothes dryers, and welders. 240V outlets have a distinct configuration with different plug shapes to prevent accidental connection to 120V equipment.
How long does dedicated circuit installation take? ▾
A straightforward dedicated circuit installation where the panel is easily accessible and the wiring run is through an open attic or crawlspace with minimal obstructions typically takes 2 to 4 hours. Installations requiring wiring through finished walls — which involves cutting access holes, fishing cable, and patching drywall — take 3 to 6 hours depending on the complexity of the wall construction and the distance from the panel to the outlet location. Longer wiring runs exceeding 75 feet require larger wire gauges to compensate for voltage drop, which adds time for more challenging cable pulls. Installations that require a subpanel, conduit work, or trenching for outdoor locations can take 4 to 8 hours or more. We always provide a specific time estimate during your free on-site evaluation based on the actual conditions of your home.
