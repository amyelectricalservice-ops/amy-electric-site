Why You Need a Licensed Electrician in Los Angeles

Electrical work is one of the most strictly regulated trades in California for good reason. Faulty wiring causes an estimated 51,000 home fires annually in the US according to the Electrical Safety Foundation International (ESFI). A licensed C-10 contractor has passed state exams, carries mandatory insurance, and must follow current California Electrical Code standards. Hiring a handyman off Craigslist to save a few hundred dollars can end up costing tens of thousands in fire damage, denied insurance claims, or code violation fines.

License Verification

How to Verify a California C-10 License

Every licensed electrical contractor in California is issued a C-10 classification by the Contractors State License Board (CSLB). You can check any license in real time using the CSLB's free online tool:

  1. Visit cslb.ca.gov and click Check a License
  2. Enter the contractor's license number
  3. Verify the license is Active, not expired or suspended
  4. Confirm the classification says C-10 Electrical Contractor
  5. Check bond and workers comp status

FAQ

Licensed Electrician FAQ

Why is it important to hire a licensed electrician in Los Angeles? ▾
California law under Business and Professions Code Section 7028 requires anyone performing electrical work for compensation to hold an active C-10 electrical contractor license issued by the Contractors State License Board (CSLB). Hiring a licensed electrician protects you with mandatory insurance coverage — including general liability and workers' compensation — that unlicensed workers simply do not carry. Licensed contractors must pass a rigorous trade exam covering the California Electrical Code, business management, and safety protocols. They are also required to maintain a bond and renew their license every two years, undergoing ongoing continuing education. If work fails to meet code or causes damage, the CSLB's Complaint Mediation process provides legal recourse through the California State License Board, which can order the contractor to correct defects or pay restitution. Unlicensed work carries no such protection, and you may be liable for injuries on your property if a handyman is hurt while working.
How do I verify an electrician's license in California? ▾
You can verify any California C-10 license instantly on the CSLB website at cslb.ca.gov through their License Check tool. Enter the licensed contractor's CSLB number and you will immediately see the verified license status, including whether the license is active, the business name and owner, bond coverage, workers compensation policy status, and any public complaints or disciplinary actions. The CSLB tool also shows the license classification (C-10 for electrical contractors), along with the license issue and expiration dates. You can also call the CSLB at (800) 321-CSLB (2752) to verify by phone. AMY Electric's C-10 license #981578 and our EVITP certification #4051604 are both active and verifiable through these channels.
What's the difference between a licensed electrician and an unlicensed handyman? ▾
An electrician with a valid C-10 license has passed the California trade examination, carries $1 million+ in general liability insurance, maintains workers compensation coverage for their employees, completes CSLB-mandated continuing education, and can legally pull permits with local building departments. An unlicensed handyman performing electrical work operates outside California law — any electrical task beyond the scope of an $500 minor repair (per the CSLB's definition) requires a C-10 license. Insurance companies routinely deny claims involving unlicensed work, leaving you financially responsible for any fire, shock, or structural damage. Unlicensed work also creates disclosure issues when selling your home, as most purchase agreements require sellers to disclose unpermitted improvements, which can delay or derail escrow.
How much does a licensed electrician cost in Los Angeles? ▾
Licensed electricians in Los Angeles typically charge between $95 and $175 for a diagnostic service call, with hourly rates ranging from $85 to $150 per hour for most residential work. Common project costs include outlet or switch replacement at $150 to $300, breaker replacement at $150 to $350, ceiling fan installation at $200 to $450, EV charger installation at $800 to $2,500, and electrical panel upgrades at $1,800 to $4,000. Rates vary based on the complexity of the job, the need for permits, and the specific city within Los Angeles — for example, permits in the City of Los Angeles (processed through LADBS) carry different fees than those in Burbank or Santa Monica. AMY Electric provides free, detailed estimates before any work begins, so you know the exact cost upfront with no hidden charges.
Do licensed electricians in Los Angeles need to pull permits? ▾
Yes — California law requires a permit for most electrical work, and only a licensed contractor can pull a permit through the local building department. In Los Angeles, permits are issued by the Los Angeles Department of Building and Safety (LADBS), while nearby cities like Burbank, Glendale, Pasadena, and Santa Monica each have their own building and safety departments with separate permitting processes. Permits are required for panel upgrades, new circuits, EV charger installations, service upgrades, rewiring, adding outlets or switches, and any structural modifications. Minor repairs like replacing an existing switch or outlet that does not involve new wiring typically do not require a permit. A licensed electrician handles every step — submitting the application, scheduling mandatory inspections, and obtaining the final sign-off.
Can a licensed electrician in Los Angeles install EV chargers and solar systems too? ▾
Yes — a C-10 licensed electrical contractor in Los Angeles is fully qualified to install Level 2 EV chargers (including Tesla Wall Connector, ChargePoint, and universal J1772 units), solar interconnection wiring, battery storage systems, and the transfer switches needed for whole-home backup generators. EV charger installation involves adding a dedicated 40–60 amp circuit from your panel, installing the charging station, and passing the LADBS or local municipal inspection. Solar work typically involves the interconnection between the inverter and your main panel, which a C-10 contractor coordinates with both LADWP and the City of Los Angeles permitting office. AMY Electric's EVITP certification further validates our expertise specifically in EV charging station installation.
What happens if an unlicensed person does electrical work on my Los Angeles home? ▾
Hiring an unlicensed person for electrical work in Los Angeles carries serious legal and financial risks. First, your homeowners insurance policy will likely deny any claim arising from unlicensed work — including fire damage, electrical shock injuries, or structural damage — because the work was performed in violation of California law. Second, when you sell your home, you must disclose unpermitted electrical modifications, which can cause buyers to walk away or demand significant price reductions. Third, if the unlicensed worker is injured on your property, you may be personally liable for medical expenses and lost wages since there is no workers compensation coverage. Finally, California law allows unlicensed contractors to walk away from defective work with no legal obligation to fix it, leaving you to pay a licensed contractor to repair and bring the work up to code.
