FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a tripping breaker dangerous? ▾
Yes — a breaker that trips frequently indicates an underlying issue that could lead to an electrical fire if ignored. While the breaker is doing its job by tripping, the root cause (overloaded circuit, short circuit, or ground fault) needs to be addressed. Continuing to reset a tripping breaker can cause overheating, damage to appliances, and in severe cases, electrical fires. Call a licensed electrician to diagnose and fix the problem rather than repeatedly resetting the breaker.
Can you replace a circuit breaker? ▾
A breaker that trips repeatedly is usually caused by one of three issues: an overloaded circuit (too many devices drawing power), a short circuit (a hot wire touching a neutral or ground), or a ground fault (current leaking to ground). Less common causes include a failing breaker, damaged wiring, or moisture in outlet boxes. We diagnose the root cause using specialized testing equipment including multimeters, clamp meters, and insulation resistance testers, then fix the underlying issue.
