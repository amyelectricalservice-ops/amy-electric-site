FAQ

Generator Transfer Switch FAQ

Do I need a permit for a generator transfer switch in Los Angeles? ▾
Yes — installing a generator transfer switch in Los Angeles requires a permit through the local building department regardless of whether you are installing a manual or automatic transfer switch. The transfer switch connects your generator directly to your home's electrical system, which means it involves modifying your electrical panel and potentially your service entrance — both of which are required by code to be permitted and inspected. The permit typically costs $100 to $300 depending on your city and covers the rough-in inspection of the transfer switch installation and the final inspection verifying proper operation, correct interlocking mechanisms, and proper labeling of generator-fed circuits. Unpermitted generator installations are dangerous — incorrect backfeeding (plugging a generator into a wall outlet) can energize utility lines and electrocute utility workers or neighbors. All of our generator transfer switch installations are fully permitted and inspected through the appropriate Los Angeles building department.
Can I install a transfer switch myself? ▾
While a homeowner with strong electrical knowledge could technically install a transfer switch under an owner-builder permit, this is one of the most dangerous electrical projects to attempt as a DIY installation. The transfer switch must be wired correctly to prevent backfeeding — a condition where generator power flows back through the panel onto the utility grid — which poses a lethal electrocution risk to utility workers and neighbors. The installation also requires correct load calculation to ensure the transfer switch is sized properly for the circuits you want to back up, proper bonding and grounding of the generator system per the California Electrical Code, and coordination with your panel's physical layout and breaker configuration. Incorrect installation can result in generator damage, electrical panel damage, voided warranty, failed inspection, and risk of fire. Given that professional transfer switch installation typically costs $800 to $1,500 and includes permits and inspections, the peace of mind is well worth the investment.
What's the difference between manual and automatic transfer switches? ▾
A manual transfer switch requires you to physically go to the transfer switch enclosure and flip the switch from utility power to generator power when an outage occurs. You also need to start the generator manually and let it warm up before switching. Manual switches are simpler, less expensive ($400 to $800 installed), and do not require any sensing electronics — they are reliable and durable because there is less to break. An automatic transfer switch (ATS) continuously monitors your utility power and automatically starts your generator within seconds of detecting a power loss, then automatically switches your home to generator power without any action from you. When utility power is restored, the ATS detects it, switches back, and runs the generator for a cool-down period before shutting it off. Automatic switches are more convenient but cost more ($1,200 to $2,500 installed) and require professional setup and programming. The right choice depends on your budget, how often you experience outages, and whether you are home during most outages.
Do I need a separate panel for my generator transfer switch? ▾
Not necessarily — there are two common approaches to connecting a generator transfer switch to your home's electrical system, and which one is right for you depends on your specific situation. The simpler approach uses a transfer switch subpanel: a small panel that feeds 6 to 12 critical circuits selected from your main panel. The transfer switch is installed between your main panel and this subpanel, allowing you to power only your essential circuits — typically refrigerator, furnace, well pump, lights, and sump pump — without the expense of powering your entire home. This approach is the most common for residential installations and costs less because the transfer switch is smaller. The second approach uses a whole-home transfer switch rated for your full service amperage (typically 200 amps), which allows you to power all circuits in your home. Whole-home transfer switches require larger generators and cost significantly more but provide full backup power for extended outages. We help you determine the right configuration based on your generator size and backup power needs.
How long does generator transfer switch installation take? ▾
A standard manual transfer switch installation — installing a 6- to 10-circuit transfer switch subpanel, running conduit between the main panel and transfer switch, wiring the selected circuits, and testing everything — typically takes 4 to 8 hours for a single day of work. An automatic transfer switch installation takes slightly longer at 6 to 10 hours because it includes programming the ATS controller, installing the generator start signal wiring, and testing the automatic power loss detection and generator start sequence. Whole-home transfer switches capable of handling 200 amps of service are more complex and typically take 1 to 2 full days because they require coordination with LADWP for service disconnection and reconnection. We always provide a specific timeline estimate during your free on-site evaluation based on your selected transfer switch type and your home's existing electrical configuration.
