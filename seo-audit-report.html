amyelectric.com

SEO review, September 10, 2026

AMY Electric is a licensed Los Angeles electrical contractor with a well-built website. The site has excellent technical SEO, comprehensive schema markup, and 76+ pages of content. The main gap is a missing backlink profile — no recorded external links pointing to the site. This report covers what is working, what to fix first, and where to focus next.

Where the site stands

The site is technically sound. Every page has proper canonical URLs, meta descriptions, Open Graph tags, and JSON-LD structured data. The sitemap lists 176 URLs with recent lastmod dates (2026-09-04). The robots.txt is well-configured — it allows AI search crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot) while blocking training crawlers. Font and image preloading hints are in place for performance.

The content is strong: 15 service pages, 16 city pages, 31 blog posts, and detailed FAQ sections on each page. Schema markup includes LocalBusiness, Electrician, FAQPage, BreadcrumbList, HowTo, and Service types — more than most competitors. The one clear gap is that the site has no recorded backlinks, which limits its ability to rank against established competitors in the local pack and organic results.

If you only do one thing

Build backlinks from local business directories and industry sources to establish domain authority.

Google uses backlinks as a primary signal of trust. A site with zero external links competes at a disadvantage against contractors who have been listed in directories, cited by local news, or linked from supplier websites for years. This is the single biggest lever for improving organic rankings.

  1. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile if not already done. Ensure the name, address, phone, and license number match the website exactly.
  2. Submit the business to these directories: Yelp (already has a listing), BBB, Angi, HomeAdvisor, Houzz, Thumbtack, and the California CSLB contractor directory. Each listing should link to amyelectric.com.
  3. Reach out to local suppliers (electrical wholesalers, EV charger brands like Tesla or ChargePoint) and ask to be listed as an authorized installer or preferred contractor.
  4. Check progress by searching site:amyelectric.com in Google after 30 days, and use a free backlink checker (Ahrefs free version or Moz Link Explorer) to verify new referring domains.

Small fixes, in order

Verify llms.txt exists low

The homepage includes <link rel="alternate" type="text/markdown" href="/llms.txt"> which is good for AI search, but the file was not fetched to verify it exists. If the file is missing or empty, AI crawlers cannot use it to understand the site.

Fix: Visit https://amyelectric.com/llms.txt. If it returns a 404, create a plain-text file describing the business, services, and contact information in markdown format.

Add dates to blog schema low

The blog posts use BlogPosting schema but the datePublished and dateModified fields were not visible in the homepage schema. Freshness signals matter for blog content.

Fix: Verify each blog post's JSON-LD includes datePublished and dateModified fields with valid ISO 8601 dates. If missing, add them to each blog post template.

Differentiate city pages low

The 16 city pages and 32 geo-service pages follow identical templates. Google may treat near-duplicate pages as thin content if the unique local information is minimal.

Fix: Add 2-3 unique sentences to each city page mentioning a specific local landmark, neighborhood, or project type relevant to that city.

Add review schema to service pages low

The homepage has aggregateRating in the Electrician schema, but individual service pages do not include Review or AggregateRating markup. This limits rich snippet opportunities.

Fix: Add the aggregateRating block to the Electrician or LocalBusiness schema on each service page. Copy the rating from the homepage schema.

Test sticky bar estimator link low

The mobile sticky bar links to index#estimator. If the estimator form is on a different page or the anchor is broken, users may not reach the form.

Fix: Test the sticky bar link on a mobile device. Verify the estimator form loads and the anchor scrolls to it correctly.

Already working

  • Comprehensive schema markup across all pages (LocalBusiness, Electrician, FAQPage, BreadcrumbList, HowTo, Service). Rich snippets in search results increase click-through rates.
  • 176 URLs in the sitemap with recent lastmod dates (2026-09-04). Fresh sitemaps help Google discover and re-crawl content faster.
  • Well-configured robots.txt that explicitly allows AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot). The site is ready for AI search visibility as these platforms grow.
  • 15 service pages with detailed pricing, process steps, and FAQ sections. Comprehensive service pages rank for long-tail commercial queries.
  • 16 city pages covering major LA neighborhoods. Local pages capture geo-modified searches like "electrician in Burbank."
  • 31 blog posts covering electrical topics, costs, and guides. Blog content captures informational traffic and builds topical authority.
  • Font, image, and CSS preloading hints for performance. Faster load times improve user experience and Core Web Vitals scores.
  • Mobile-responsive design with sticky call bar on mobile. Most local searches happen on mobile; easy tap-to-call drives conversions.
  • Consistent NAP (name, address, phone) across all pages and schema. NAP consistency is a ranking factor for local pack results.
  • SSL certificate active, HTTPS enforced. HTTPS is a ranking signal and required for trust.
