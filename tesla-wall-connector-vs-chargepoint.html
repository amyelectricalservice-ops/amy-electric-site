Tesla vs ChargePoint: the Five Differences That Matter

Connector first: the Tesla Wall Connector uses Tesla's NACS plug — perfect if every EV in the garage is a Tesla, awkward for guests with J1772 cars unless you keep an adapter handy. The ChargePoint Home Flex ships with the universal J1772 connector that fits every non-Tesla EV sold in America, plus any Tesla using the small adapter that comes with the car. Mixed household or planning a second, non-Tesla EV? Go universal. All-Tesla driveway? The Wall Connector is sleeker and cheaper.

Speed second: both max out at 48 amps on a 60-amp circuit — about 37 to 44 miles of range per hour depending on the vehicle — so neither is meaningfully faster in a home garage. Both need the same thing from your house: a load calculation, a dedicated circuit, and usually a permit. Installation runs $500 to $1,200 in Los Angeles for either unit; the Tesla unit itself ($475, bought from Tesla) typically costs less than the ChargePoint. Smart features are a wash — both schedule off-peak charging and track energy in their apps. Our recommendation: Tesla-only homes take the Wall Connector; everyone else takes the universal station, and renters or anyone wanting portability takes a NEMA 14-50 outlet ($350–$900 installed) instead of either hardwired unit.

FAQ

Tesla Wall Connector vs ChargePoint — FAQ

Which is better: Tesla Wall Connector or ChargePoint Home Flex? ▾
For Tesla-only households, the Wall Connector: lower unit cost ($475), sleeker cable management, and native Tesla app integration. For mixed or future EV households, the ChargePoint Home Flex: universal J1772 fits every non-Tesla EV, with comparable 48-amp speed and scheduling. Charging speed is effectively tied — both max 48 amps on a 60-amp circuit.
How much does each cost installed in Los Angeles? ▾
Installed cost runs $500 to $1,200 in Los Angeles for either station, including the dedicated circuit, wiring, mounting, LADBS permit, inspection, and commissioning. The Tesla unit ($475 from Tesla) usually costs less than the ChargePoint unit; total project cost ends up within a few hundred dollars either way. NEMA 14-50 outlets run $350 to $900 installed for comparison.
Can a ChargePoint charge a Tesla, and vice versa? ▾
A ChargePoint (J1772) charges any Tesla with the small adapter included with every Tesla. A Tesla Wall Connector charges only Teslas natively — non-Tesla EVs need a third-party NACS-to-J1772 adapter. The Tesla Universal Wall Connector bridges both with its built-in adapter dock.
Do both need a 60-amp circuit and a permit? ▾
Both deliver full speed on a 60-amp circuit and can be commissioned lower (40–50 amps) where panel capacity is tight. Los Angeles requires an LADBS electrical permit for either hardwired station — we handle permitting and inspection on every install at no hassle to you.
Which charges faster at home? ▾
Neither, practically: both cap at 48 amps continuous (about 11.5 kW), adding roughly 37 to 44 miles of range per hour depending on vehicle efficiency. Home charging speed is set by the circuit and the car's onboard charger, so both stations fill any EV overnight.
What about renters or people who might move? ▾
Choose a NEMA 14-50 outlet ($350–$900 installed) with a portable mobile connector instead of a hardwired station. You take the charger when you move, the outlet adds value for the next tenant, and a future hardwired station can reuse the same circuit.
Are you licensed to install both in Los Angeles? ▾
Yes — AMY Electric holds California C-10 license #981578, verifiable at cslb.ca.gov, plus EVITP certification #4051604 covering all major charging equipment. We install Tesla, ChargePoint, JuiceBox, and universal stations across Greater Los Angeles, permitted and inspected, backed by our workmanship warranty.
