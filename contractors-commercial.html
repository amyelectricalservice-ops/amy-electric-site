Who we work with

A subcontractor that answers the phone and shows up

General contractors carrying a schedule, property managers holding a tenant complaint, and business owners opening or expanding a space all need the same three things from an electrical subcontractor: a scope they can price against, a permit path that is named up front, and a crew that does not disappear mid-job. That is the work this page is about.

  • General contractors — rough-in and finish electrical on TI and remodel scopes, with a written scope and a schedule you can build around.
  • Property managers and HOAs — service and panel work, common-area lighting, troubleshooting, and EV charging for tenant parking.
  • Commercial tenants and owners — restaurant, retail, office, and light-industrial electrical, from a single dedicated circuit to a service upgrade.

What we take on

  • Service and panel upgrades — 200A residential, 400A and three-phase commercial, subpanels, and load calculations.
  • Tenant improvements — new circuits, receptacle and lighting layouts, emergency and exit lighting, and final connections.
  • Dedicated circuits — kitchen equipment, HVAC, compressors, servers, and shop machinery.
  • Lighting — retrofits and new installation, including controls and warehouse high-bay.
  • EV charging and fleet — Level 2 stations, load management, and multi-tenant charging design.
  • Troubleshooting — intermittent faults, hot panels, nuisance tripping, and failed equipment.

How a commercial job runs

1. Walkthrough and scope. We look at the service, the existing distribution, and the access route before quoting. The written scope names what is included and what is excluded — trenching, patching, painting, and equipment supplied by others are called out rather than assumed.

2. Permit responsibility, named. The permit is either ours or yours, and the proposal says which. In the City of Los Angeles that usually means LADBS for the electrical permit and LADWP for meter and service coordination; in Burbank, Glendale, or Santa Monica the municipal utility and building department have their own steps.

3. Schedule and site rules. Work windows, shutdowns, parking, loading access, and any building security requirements are agreed before the crew arrives. Certificates of insurance and W-9 are available on request.

4. Changes in writing. A change order is priced before the work, not after. Nothing gets built on a verbal maybe.

5. Close-out. Panel schedules are labelled, circuits are identified, and inspection results are documented so the next trade can work in the same building without guessing.

Credentials and coverage

AMY Electric holds an active California C-10 Electrical Contractor license (#981578) and EVITP certification (#4051604), carries a CSLB-filed contractor bond and general liability coverage, and pulls permits through the authority having jurisdiction for each project.
